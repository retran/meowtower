# 0460. Every gap the addendum 2 records still leave open settles by ADR-0360's defaults with the newer addendum 2 decision owning its own capability, rejected findings stay as records, and eight requirements get replacements

## Decision

On 2026-09-29 the owner said "settle them" about the open findings the addendum 2 specifications recorded, after telling research to decide conflicts itself. The approved decisions, the approved research and the live specifications hold 242 findings under `## Open findings` and `## Open review findings`. I read each against the records it names. 134 are findings their author rejected for a valid reason, most of them requests for reasons in a specification, which spec rule S8 forbids, and they stay in their files as records; every finding ADR-0370 classed as rejected keeps that class. 24 are already settled: by the current text of an approved record, by RES-4280's rerun of the simulations, or by a reversal condition that already names the response. I settle the other 84 in the 82 entries below, because two pairs of findings ask one question. Each entry names the section it closes, the records it touches, the rule that now holds and its reason.

I applied ADR-0360's defaults in its order. A rule that keeps her measurement fair, her data on the Mac and her screens free of clocks wins. The daily quests and daily rewards stay every game day once their system opens. The decision that owns a capability wins a detail of it, and where an addendum 2 decision, ADR-0380 to ADR-0450, and an older decision disagree on an addendum 2 capability, the addendum 2 decision wins, because it was written against the addendum with the older decision in front of it. An approved requirement wins over a decision unless it can't hold, and then a replacement, REQ-7500 to REQ-7514, carries the rule. A pure gap gets the smallest rule a test can check. Every entry on the Dutch probe stays conditional on the owner amending the Russian-only rule of `CLAUDE.md`, as ADR-0430 already makes the whole probe.

## Why

A finding a specification can't settle blocks the epic step for its part, because an epic built on an open rule builds one side of it. The owner said "settle them", and ADR-0360 set the defaults for doing so; I kept them so the three records read the same way, and added the rule the brief for this record set on 2026-09-29 that the newer addendum 2 decision wins its own capability. No entry turned on that rule alone: entry 33 follows the approved requirement over both decisions, and entry 53 is settled by what each event schema is for.

Most of what remains isn't open. 134 findings were rejected for valid reasons: requests for reasons in a specification, which S8 forbids, requests to change what an approved decision or requirement already sets, and preferences on a record's form. Deleting them would lose the author's reason, and the approver reads them, so they stay. 24 more are settled by text the owner already approved, most of them notes that a round's fixes went unreviewed, in records that are now approved.

The 82 settlements are of three kinds. Most are gaps: an event with no payload, a route with no reply, a count with no denominator. There I chose the smallest rule a test can check, so a later decision can replace it without undoing much. Some are readings of records that contradict each other, such as entry 33, where two decisions count eight screens and a requirement nine; there I followed the approved requirement. Eight touch approved requirements that can't hold as written, and each gets a replacement the owner approves on its own: REQ-6668 and REQ-6818 say less than their decisions need, REQ-0980, REQ-5636 and REQ-6936 name a case their decisions exclude, REQ-6426 can't hold across a restart and REQ-7192 on a day with too few pairs, and REQ-7360's bar passes a failing hold half the time (RES-4280).

The strongest objection is that several entries invent behaviour no research recorded: the role timeouts and token ceilings (entry 20), the 10 CSS px tap threshold (entry 62), a context on every probe pair (entry 69) and the dead hours at the month's reset (entry 23). A wrong number in entry 20 cuts a reply short on her screen, and a wrong threshold in entry 62 turns her pan into a tap on a source. I kept each small and gave entries 20, 23 and 62 a reversal condition below, and each entry can be rejected alone, which reopens only its finding. To reject one before approving, the owner deletes the entry's text and its lines in Consequences and Amends, lists it under What this does not settle, and leaves its number empty so the other numbers still hold.
