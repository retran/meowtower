# Scenarios and arcs can follow acts, a central conflict, beats and turning values, and code can check most of that shape

## Summary

The owner asked on 2026-11-01 that scenarios and arcs use the best screenwriting practice. Most of the structure can be stored as fields and checked by code: three acts, the turning points, a stated conflict, a dramatic question and a value that turns in each encounter. What can't be checked by a field goes to the critic. A real run showed why: its critic found illusory branches and setups with no payoff, which a shape helps prevent. The books weren't read at first hand. This covers structure, and it doesn't show that a child enjoys the result.

## The question

Which craft rules can a scenario hold as data, and which stay judgement? A fixed formula can flatten a story, and Dramatron's documentation warns that top-down structure doesn't suit everyone, so the shape is a skeleton and the Master remains free inside it.

## Method

Read on 2026-11-01: the pages under Sources. Nothing ran on a real model after the change.

## Findings

### Three acts name the shape

[Read] Wikipedia describes setup with an inciting incident and a first plot point that makes life permanently different and raises the dramatic question, rising action in which the hero must grow, and a resolution whose climax answers the question.

### A scene turns a value

[Snippet] McKee defines a scene as action through conflict that turns a value-charged condition, and holds that a scene with no change is empty. A beat is an action and reaction, a sequence builds to a stronger moment, and an act peaks in a major reversal.

### Beat sheets add named points

[Snippet] Snyder's sheet refines three acts into 15 beats, and sources confirm the catalyst. Its midpoint, all-is-lost and finale come from general knowledge of it, which I didn't read at source.

### A circle states change in the hero

[Read] Harmon's circle runs you, need, go, search, find, take, return, change: the hero pays a price and comes back changed.

## Conclusions

1. A scenario states its central conflict and its dramatic question.
2. Each encounter holds an act, a beat, a tension and a value that turns.
3. Code checks the three acts, the turning points, that acts never go backwards, that values turn, that the climax is the peak and that every path passes it.
4. An arc holds the same shape across its adventures.
5. The critic judges what a field can't prove: whether a turn truly reverses, whether the climax answers the question, and whether setups pay off.
