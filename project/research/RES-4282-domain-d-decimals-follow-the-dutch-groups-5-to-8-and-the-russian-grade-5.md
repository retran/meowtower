# Decimals enter the Dutch programme at group 5 and the Russian at grade 5, so the Dutch base leads on every decimal topic except rounding

## Summary

The Dutch goals set the decimal ranges. D1 starts at group 5 with money to two places. The first decimal addition goal found is at group 6, and no group 5 decimal addition goal turned up. The Russian programme has no decimal fractions in grades 1 to 4. It adds them in grade 5, which is Dutch group 7 when a Russian grade n is read as Dutch group n + 2, so the Dutch base is first or level on every decimal topic except rounding, where Russian grade 5 comes one year before Dutch group 8. D7, the order of operations with fractions and decimals, has no Dutch or Russian goal in the text read.

About 28 distinct task forms appear in the sources. D1 has enough for ten or more templates. D2 to D7 have fewer than ten each from what was read, so those subtypes need forms from sources not yet read. Several groups in the graph are not confirmed by a goal at that group, and this record lists them; it does not change the graph.

## The question

What must the templates for domain D (nodes D1 to D7) ask, and at which Dutch group, so that the Dutch programme is the base, the Russian programme's additions are marked as extensions, and the forms cover the widest variety of tasks? The owner's rule: the Dutch programme for groups 1 to 8 is the base, and a Russian grade n is Dutch group n + 2.

## Method

A research agent drafted this record from the extracted source texts, and the main session checked a sample of its quotes against those texts: the group 5 money goal (tussendoelen lines 537 to 540) and the grade 5 decimal list (federal programme, grades 5 to 9, lines 524 to 528) match.

Read: the Dutch tussendoelen (Getalbegrip and Bewerkingen blocks, including the decimal rows) and the SLO concretisation of 1F and 1S (Domein Getallen), at the lines cited; the federal programme grade sections for grades 1 to 4 and grades 5 and 6; the Moro grade 2 (1977) passage on place value, and a search of the Moro grade 1 (1990) text, which has no matches; the Vilenkin 2024 decimal rules (modern, not Soviet). The MCCME 2021 and 2023 papers and both Dutch Kangoeroe reports were searched for decimal terms and only the matching problems read. The Tartu Kangaroo papers were searched and the first lines of the grade 3 to 4 paper read; they hold no decimal item.

Node meanings and catalogue rows came from RES-0800 and RES-1200, which are project files and not primary sources; they are used only to name subtypes and levels.

Not obtained: the Russian textbooks for grades 5 and 6 (only the programme outline was read), a readable Soviet decimal text (Moro 1977 and 1990 contain none), and any Dutch Kangoeroe item text with decimals (the two reports hold result tables, not items).

## Findings

### D1 place value: the Dutch goal sets group 5

Group 5 is the first decimal goal in the tussendoelen: "weet wat de komma betekent in geldbedragen (met twee cijfers achter de komma) en kan decimale getallen in de context van geld uitspreken en schrijven" (tussendoelen lines 537 to 540). This matches the graph's group 5.

### D2 addition and subtraction: not located at group 5

The first decimal addition goal found is group 6: "kan twee of meer geldbedragen bij elkaar optellen en van elkaar aftrekken (bv.: € 8,90 + € 1,10 + € 7,10 + € 2,90 op kassabonnen)" (tussendoelen lines 1046 to 1047, group 6 column by its header at 1026 to 1027). The group 5 Bewerkingen lines read (985 to 1018) are about whole numbers. The graph's group 5 for D2 is therefore unconfirmed, and the group 5 money goal covers only meaning and ordering.

### D3 shift by 10: a decimal goal was not located

The Dutch goals give multiplication by 10 and 100 for whole numbers at group 7: "begrijpt vermenigvuldigen en delen met factor 10 en 100 en kan dit uitleggen en toepassen (bv.: 80 x 90 =; 800 x 90 =...)" (tussendoelen lines 1122 to 1125, group 7 column). A decimal factor appears only in a concretisation example list, "1000 x 2,5; 10 x 0,45; 75 : 10; 0,25 x 100; 100 x 4,5" (concretisering line 1476 to 1477), whose level column the text extraction does not keep.

### D4 multiplication: the Dutch goal is at group 7

"kan vlot vermenigvuldigen en delen met eenvoudige decimale getallen (bv.: 4 x 2,25; 12 x 3,99 = 12 x 4 - 12 x 0,01; 35 : 2,5 = 0,5...)" (tussendoelen lines 1212 to 1213 and 1225, group 7 column). The concretisation adds "5 uur werken voor € 5,75 per uur, hoeveel is dat in totaal?" (line 1637, level unclear in the extraction). The Dutch goal is therefore one year after the graph's group 6.

### D5 division: group 7 or 1S, unsettled

The Dutch division rows are "35 : 2,5" (tussendoelen line 1225, group 7 column) and "Rond het antwoord af op een getal met twee cijfers achter de komma" (concretisering lines 1979 to 1985, level unclear). The graph's group 6 for D5 is not supported by a decimal division goal at group 6 in the lines read.

### D6 conversion: the Dutch goals are in the group 7 and group 8 columns

Group 7: "begrijpt de relatie tussen breuken en decimale getallen en kan veel voorkomende breuken en decimale getallen in elkaar omzetten (bv.: 1/5 = 0,2)" (tussendoelen lines 697 to 700). Group 8: "kan (samengestelde) breuken omzetten in decimale getallen en omgekeerd ... (bv.: 4/5 = 0,8; 2 4/5 = 2,8; 7/10 = 0,7; 0,22 = 11/50)" (lines 691 to 694). The 1F concretisation has a simple conversion, "Hoe kun je 3,7 als breuk schrijven? 3 7/10" (concretisering line 2682), so simple conversion is a 1F skill. The graph's group 6 is not confirmed by a conversion goal at group 6.

### D7 order of operations with decimals and fractions: the sources are silent

No Dutch goal read names the order of operations with decimals and fractions. A search for rekenvolgorde, voorrang and volgorde van bewerkingen found no such row. D7 stays at stretch until a goal is found.

### Rounding decimals: the Dutch goal is at group 8

"kan decimale afronden op honderdsten, tienden en een geheel getal" (tussendoelen lines 623 to 624, group 8 column). The 1S row adds rounding "volgens de afrondingsregels, ook in de context van geld" (lines 630 to 631).

### The Russian programme has no decimal fractions in grades 1 to 4

In the grade 1 to 4 text the only "десятичный состав" is "Числа в пределах 100: чтение, запись, десятичный состав, сравнение" (federal programme, grades 1 to 4, line 257), the tens-and-ones composition of whole numbers. Grade 3 has "разностное, кратное" comparison (line 443), and grade 4 has "Умножение и деление на 10, 100, 1000" (line 564). These are the nearest Russian matches for D3.

### The Russian programme teaches decimals in grade 5 and grade 6

Grade 5: "Десятичная запись дробей. Представление десятичной дроби в виде обыкновенной. Изображение десятичных дробей точками на числовой прямой. Сравнение десятичных дробей. Арифметические действия с десятичными дробями. Округление десятичных дробей." (grades 5 to 9, lines 524 to 528). Grade 5 also teaches "Римская нумерация как пример непозиционной системы счисления" (lines 495 to 496), an extension with no Dutch counterpart.

Grade 6: "Представление десятичной дроби в виде обыкновенной дроби ... Выражение процентов десятичными дробями" (lines 579 to 587), and "Арифметические действия и числовые выражения с обыкновенными и десятичными дробями" (lines 581 to 582). Logic problems solved "перебором всех возможных вариантов" (line 604) are an extension of the reasoning kind.

With the mapping, Russian grade 5 is group 7 and grade 6 is group 8. The Russian decimal arithmetic and number line fall at the same age as the Dutch group 7 multiplication goal, and the Russian decimal rounding is one year before the Dutch group 8 goal.

### Soviet-era evidence: none for decimals

The Moro grade 2 text (1977) has place-value composition of whole numbers: "Единицы пишутся на первом месте, считая справа налево (они называются единицами первого разряда), десятки — на втором ... Отсутствие единиц какого-либо разряда обозначается цифрой 0." (moro2-1977 lines 11531 to 11536). It contains no decimal fraction. The Moro grade 1 text (1990) has no match for decimal terms, comma, rounding or place value. The Vilenkin 2024 text is modern and not Soviet; its rules read "Чтобы сложить (вычесть) десятичные дроби, надо: 1) уравнять в этих дробях количество знаков после запятой" (vilenkin6-2024 lines 814 to 817) and "отделить в ответе запятой столько цифр справа, сколько их стоит после запятой в множителях вместе" (lines 829 to 830).

### Olympiad items with decimals all lie above the mapped range

MCCME 2023, grade 7, task 7.1: "Решите ребус C,BA + A,AA = B,A", with the solution step "Разряд сотых может исчезнуть при суммировании" (oly-mccme-2023 lines 652 to 664). MCCME 2023, grade 7, task 7.2: a bank deposit with decimal multipliers, "3000 ⋅ 1,2 ⋅ 0,9 = 3240" (lines 694 to 697), which touches the percent domain. MCCME 2021, grade 9, task 9.2: "Ответ запишите в виде десятичной дроби. Ответ: 0,4." (oly-mccme-2021 lines 1109 to 1114). All three are extensions.

The Dutch Kangoeroe reports 396 and 397 hold decimals only in answer-share tables and a fee in the report text; they are not items. The Tartu grade 3 to 4 paper has no decimal item, and the preecolier paper has no decimal match. No Kangaroo item for D1 to D7 was found.

### The Dutch concretisation has the richest task forms

Each form with the line it comes from; level is marked where the extraction keeps the column.

- Value of one digit: "Wat is die 6 achter de komma waard in 1,65 meter?" (line 4428).
- Money as a decimal: "Hoe schrijf je '125 eurocent' in een kommagetal?" (line 3539).

- Explain a rule: "Onder elkaar optellen en aftrekken: met kommagetallen moeten de komma's onder elkaar. Is dat altijd zo? Waarom moet dat?" (lines 2464 to 2466).

### Russian forms that add to the Dutch list

Grade 5 adds decimals as points on the line and the comparison and rounding of decimals (grades 5 to 9, lines 524 to 528). Grade 6 adds the conversion of a decimal to a fraction and back (lines 579 to 580), percentages as decimals (lines 585 to 586), and the logic task solved by trying all cases (line 604).

### Where the sources are silent or conflict

- D2: no group 5 decimal addition goal was found.
- D3: no decimal times 10 or divided by 10 goal was located, only a concretisation example list at an unclear level.
- D5 and D6: the graph's group 6 is not supported by a decimal goal at group 6.
- D7: no Dutch or Russian goal was read.
- The 1F and 1S column for concretisation lines 1476, 1637 and 1979 to 1985 is not preserved by the text extraction, so those rows are attributed only by their wording.
- RES-4281 says the number line is taught "zowel precies als ongeveer" for decimals; the concretisation example at line 1043 uses a whole number, so that is not confirmed for decimals.

## Conclusions

1. Each D subtype's core templates use the numbers and contexts that the Dutch goal for their group allows, with the group stated on each template: D1 at group 5 (money with two places), D4 at group 7, D6 at group 7 for simple conversions and group 8 for rounding.
2. D2 stays at group 6 for addition until a group 5 decimal addition goal is found, and the graph's group 5 is recorded as unconfirmed.
3. D5 and D6 carry their group as "group 7 or 1S, unsettled" until the concretisation column is read in the source layout.
4. D7 stays at stretch and is marked as having no Dutch goal in the sources read.
5. The Russian rounding of decimals at grade 5 and the Roman numerals at grade 5 are extensions, marked with grade and passage.
6. The olympiad items (MCCME 2023 tasks 7.1 and 7.2, MCCME 2021 task 9.2) are extensions, and their templates name the grade and the solution step.
7. D1 has at least ten distinct forms in the list above (reading, writing, ordering, selecting, placing, rounding, value of a digit, comma placement). D2 to D7 each have fewer than ten forms in the sources read, so each needs forms from Russian grade 5 and 6 textbooks or forms written by the author, and the record for that subtype names which.
8. The answer contract needs a set kind (select every number that fits), a pair kind (match a decimal with a fraction), an acceptance distance for a point placed "ongeveer" on a line, and a way to state the position of a comma, because a digit count fits an integer answer only when the position is stated as a count. The set kind, the pair kind and the distance are approved; the position of a comma is not yet decided.
