# Domain N follows the Dutch groups 1 to 8 as its base, the Russian grades 1 to 6 add a few extensions, and the Dutch standard itself allows reasoning tasks

## Summary

The Dutch programme is the base of the templates and the Russian programme adds extensions to it. The two agree on what number sense is and on its order: counting and comparing to 20 and 100, place value to 1,000, large numbers to a million and beyond, and from the last two years rounding, the number line and negative numbers. The Dutch goals run from group 2 to group 8, with group 1 sharing the group 2 goals. The Russian federal programme covers grades 1 to 4 in one text and grades 5 and 6 in another. Domain N's 15 subtypes fit inside that span, and the stretch node N8, arithmetic with negative numbers, sits beyond the Dutch primary standard and inside Russian grade 6.

The ages line up: a Russian grade n is the Dutch group n + 2, so grades 1 to 6 sit on groups 3 to 8 and groups 1 and 2 have no Russian primary counterpart. Two findings change the template plan. The Dutch goals themselves include reasoning questions about numbers, so templates of that kind are inside the standard, not an extra. And the existing answer kinds can't express several forms the sources use. This record covers domain N only; each other domain gets a record of its own before its templates are written.

## The question

What must the templates of domain N ask, at which group, so that they cover the Dutch groups 1 to 8 and the Russian grades 1 to 6 with the widest variety of task forms? The owner's rule is that the Dutch programme is the base and the Russian programme extends it, and the assumption is that ten templates for each subtype and answer kind, and twenty or more where the subtype allows, are enough; this record tests whether the forms exist to fill that many without repeating one form with different numbers.

## Method

I read these sources in full text, extracted with `pdftotext`: the SLO tussendoelen rekenen-wiskunde po 2017 (the Getalbegrip tables, groups 2 to 8), the SLO concretisering of reference levels 1F and 1S (Domein Getallen, part A and B, October 2011), and the federal working programme for mathematics, grades 1 to 4 and grades 5 to 9, base level (edsoo.ru, July 2025). I did not read the Soviet-era textbooks of Moro, Bantova and Vilenkin themselves: no scan or text of them could be fetched, and the web tools returned only teachers' derived plans. The federal programme is the legal successor of that tradition, and I treat it as the Russian primary source. The Kangaroo and school-stage olympiad papers were located and not yet read: the Dutch Kangoeroe papers on w4kangoeroe.nl, the Russian-language Kangaroo papers hosted by the University of Tartu, and the MCCME school-stage papers with solutions at olympiads.mccme.ru.

## Findings

### The Dutch goals set a number range for each group

The tussendoelen, Getalbegrip, whole numbers: end of group 2, counting to 20; group 3, to 100, reading and saying them, placing numbers to 20 on a number line; group 4, to 100 with place value, splitting into tens and ones, even and odd, placing on a number line; group 5, to 1,000, with rounding to hundreds (879 to 900); group 6, to ±100,000 with rounding to hundreds and thousands; group 7, to ±1 million with rounding "where the goal sets the accuracy" (371,389 to 370,000 and not 400,000); group 8, whole numbers of any size, with 1,425,000 as "ruim 1,4 miljoen". Group 1 has no goals of its own: the document says the end-of-group-2 goals belong to both school years.

### The Dutch goals set decimals from group 5 and a notation for each

Group 5, decimals only as money with two places (€ 7,05 is more than € 6,95); group 6, one and two places, splitting 3,25 = 3 + 0,2 + 0,05; group 7, three places, and more places mean finer accuracy (2,16 m against 2,1); group 8, rounding to hundredths, tenths and whole numbers. The 1S concretisation adds meaning for "more complex" decimals such as 0,384.

### The Dutch standard asks the child to reason about numbers, with examples

Every group lists "kan kritisch denken en redeneren over ... in probleemsituaties" with a worked example: group 3, "Josien en Aref wonen allebei op nummer 12, maar niet bij elkaar in huis. Hoe kan dat?"; group 4, "Zijn er precies 100 sommen met het antwoord 100?"; group 5, "Hoeveel getallen zijn er tot 1000 met één 9 erin?"; group 6, "Welke steden hebben ongeveer 100.000 inwoners?". These are olympiad-style tasks, and they sit in the standard.

### The concretisation gives 1F and 1S concrete limits

For comparing and ordering: 1F whole numbers under ±100,000 and simple decimals, 1S under ±1,000,000 and decimals, with house prices, years and lengths as contexts. For rounding: 1F whole numbers to about ±10,000 (20,000) with the goal setting the accuracy, 1S to ±1 billion and decimals to whole numbers in context ("77,6 dozen berekend, dus 78 dozen kopen"). For the number line: placing whole numbers and decimals "zowel precies als ongeveer" ("waar ligt 598 ongeveer", "tussen welke duizendtallen ligt 2789"). For large numbers: 1F reads "zeventien miljoen" as digits and knows "miljoen"; 1S knows that a billion is a thousand million. For splitting: 1F "6400 = 4 x 100 + 6 x ...", 1S "3,4 miljoen = 3 x ... + 4 x ...", and "vul aan tot een miljoen". For estimating: "globaal beredeneren van uitkomsten". For temperature: 1F "vijf graden boven nul", and 1S "wat betekent: het is -5 °C?".

### The Russian federal programme sets the same order by grade

Grade 1: numbers 1 to 9 and to 20, reading, writing, comparing, "увеличение (уменьшение) числа на несколько единиц". Grade 2: numbers to 100, decimal composition, comparing, "разностное сравнение чисел". Grade 3: numbers to 1,000, sums of place-value terms, "кратное сравнение чисел". Grade 4: numbers to a million, "поразрядное сравнение, упорядочение", the number larger or smaller by a given count of place units or a given number of times, multiplication and division by 10, 100, 1000. Rounding, the coordinate line and negatives are absent from grades 1 to 4 in this text.

### The Russian programme teaches rounding, the number line and negatives in grades 5 and 6

Grade 5: natural numbers shown as points on the coordinate line, comparison, "округление натуральных чисел", sums of place-value terms, decimals as points on the line, comparing and rounding decimals, Roman numerals as a non-positional system. Grade 6: "положительные и отрицательные числа, целые числа, модуль числа, изображение чисел на координатной прямой, числовые промежутки, сравнение чисел, арифметические действия", the rectangular coordinate plane, "оценка и прикидка, округление результата", and logic problems solved by trying all variants.

### The sources differ in a few places the templates must respect

The Dutch standard teaches rounding from group 5 and the Russian programme from grade 5, about the same age, so rounding is common ground. The Dutch standard puts decimals before large numbers (decimals as money in group 5), and the Russian programme puts decimals in grade 5 after large numbers. A temperature below zero is a meaning goal in Dutch 1S (group 8) and an arithmetic topic in Russian grade 6. Arithmetic with negative numbers is outside the Dutch primary standard, which agrees with the graph placing N8 at stretch.

### The Russian programme adds a few topics the Dutch standard does not require

Against the Dutch base, the Russian text adds, for the number sense of domain N: comparison by "how many times" (кратное сравнение, grade 3), multiplication and division by 10, 100 and 1000 as a number-sense step (grade 4), Roman numerals (grade 5), the rectangular coordinate plane (grade 6), and arithmetic with negative numbers and the absolute value (grade 6), which the Dutch primary standard leaves to secondary school. These are extensions: the Dutch goals do not forbid them, and the Russian text gives them an age.

### The existing answer kinds cover most forms and miss four

SPC-0040 lists integer, decimal, fraction, mixed, quotientRemainder, time, point, compare, choice, grid, order, steps, equation and region. The sources use forms with no kind: select every number that fits (a set), give the number before and after (an ordered pair), name a missing digit in a column of a number (an integer works), complete a split such as 6400 = 4 x 100 + 6 x ... (an integer works), say how many numbers fit a rule (an integer works), and build the largest or smallest number from given digit cards (an integer works). Only the set and the ordered pair need a new kind; a range "between X and Y" can be a choice. A tolerance rule is also missing for "ongeveer" tasks on a number line, where a placed point is right within a stated distance.

## Conclusions

1. Domain N's templates conform to the Dutch programme first: each template carries a group range, and its numbers and contexts stay inside that group's range in the tussendoelen and, for the end of primary school, the 1F and 1S concretisation.
2. A topic taken from the Russian programme and not required by the Dutch one is an extension: its templates are marked as extensions, name the Russian grade and the passage, and never raise the group range the Dutch standard sets for the core topic.
3. Group 1 is covered by the templates written for group 2, because the Dutch goals give group 1 no goals of its own.
4. Each subtype has at least ten templates, twenty or more where the subtype has that many distinct forms, and they use different task forms, drawn from reading, writing, comparing, ordering, placing on a line, splitting and composing, rounding with a stated purpose, estimating, finding a missing part, finding a mistake, choosing the correct statement among four, and a reasoning task about the subtype; a form counts once however its numbers vary.
5. At least two of each subtype's templates are reasoning tasks of the kind the Dutch standard lists and the olympiads set, where the subtype allows one, and the subtype's research record names the source of each.
6. The answer contract gains a set kind (select every correct option) and an ordered-pair kind (two numbers), and an acceptance distance for a point placed "ongeveer" on a line.
7. Rounding templates state their purpose, because the Dutch goals make the purpose set the accuracy.
8. N8, arithmetic with negative numbers, stays at stretch and is an extension, and N7's thermometer templates read and compare temperatures without arithmetic until the order subtype reaches 1S.
9. Before its templates are written, every domain gets a record like this one that names the passages it rests on, and the Soviet-era textbook sources are added when a readable copy is found.
