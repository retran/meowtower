# The Dutch probe can tell a maths gap from a language gap only as a post-MVP experiment the owner unlocks, with the two Dutch presentations in a fixed order, a card tap counted as help, and about 20 observations a presentation before any gap is read

## Summary

The owner's addendum 2 of 2026-09-28, item 6, adds a Dutch diagnostic probe. The engine builds families of parallel tasks on one maths construction and shows each family in up to five presentations: bare, Russian text, Dutch text, Dutch text after the unfamiliar words are explained, and a table or chart. The report compares them. The published evidence supports the design's premise and warns about its reading. Language skill weighs more on context problems than on bare sums in Dutch primary pupils, and a glossary closes only 11 % to 21 % of a second-language gap, so a gap that stays after the word cards is not proof of a maths gap. The approved record conflicts with the item in ten places: the Russian-only rule, ADR-0290's rule against a Cito layout, `PLANNER_MODEL` as a play role, the Russian-only forbidden-word gate, RES-3900's rule that T reaches play through the Guardians, ADR-0250's button, four options and full phases on every T problem, ADR-0080's twin, the bridge's share, the language-risk limit and the canon. The largest is that every Dutch sentence beyond the bridge's 30 to 50 keywords breaks the Russian-only rule, which only the owner changes. At about 4 probe tasks a game day for 3 to 4 weeks, each presentation of the overall table gets about 20 observations, enough to see a 28-point gap and too few to see a 10-point one; a per-node cell gets about 3 and must read «мало данных» (too little data). Research decided the twelve open questions on 2026-09-28, and its main choice is to build the probe after the MVP, once the owner amends the Russian-only rule. This record covers item 6 and its acceptance test 4, and leaves the profile, retention, transfer, the quadrants and the hypotheses to the records on items 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8.

## The question

What must the game, its data and the approved record do so that the probe answers "maths, or access to maths through language and format", with the same force in either direction? The owner's addendum 2 is the instruction, dated 2026-09-28, and outranks addendum 1 and the approved record where they disagree. The constraints are the owner's standing rules: fair measurement, nothing new leaving the Mac without a decision that allows it, no clock the player sees, and new observation streams kept out of «сама» (on her own).

The addendum assumes that the difference between presentations measures language and format, and that the five presentations differ in nothing else. That assumption carries the whole probe, so I challenge it before any option. Three things besides language change between presentations. The first is practice: she meets the same construction up to five times, and each meeting teaches her something. The second is what a word card does to the next presentation: once she has read the card, the words stay learned, so presentation 3 after presentation 4 isn't the same condition as presentation 3 before it. The third is the text itself: a Dutch text a model wrote can be harder or stranger than the Dutch she meets at school, and then the gap measures the model's Dutch. Each of these moves the gap in a known direction, and most of them make a language problem look larger than it is. So the question has two parts: which approved rules the probe breaks, and what keeps its gaps honest.

The addendum also assumes the probe belongs in the game now. The Russian-only rule, the MVP scope and the owner's own addendum 1 place Dutch task text in the deferred Dutch layer, so the timing is part of the question too.

## Findings

### The addendum sets five presentations, Dutch letters, word cards, offline texts, separate streams and a report

The owner's addendum 2, item 6, read 2026-09-28, sets these rules. For 1 to 2 chosen templates on each of T1 to T4, P, F, M and the Sources track, the engine builds a family of parallel tasks on one construction, with different numbers and the same difficulty features. The five presentations are a bare sum, a Russian word problem, a Dutch contextsom "in Cito's style", the same Dutch problem after the unfamiliar words are explained, and a table or chart "in a Cito-like format" where the node suits it. Presentation 4 is a second show with new numbers after she opened the word cards or was shown them.

The volume is about 4 probe tasks a day for 3 to 4 weeks, then 1 to 2 a day. Presentations of one family fall on different days, and their order is balanced, "not always bare first", so the probe doesn't measure practice. The Dutch tasks arrive as «письма с Большой земли» (letters from the Mainland), messages from a neighbouring land where people speak another language, which the heroine deciphers, in the usual task window. She can touch a word in a Dutch task to open its bridge card, and a touch "moves the attempt to presentation 4 for measurement".

Dutch texts are prepared offline. `PLANNER_MODEL` writes them after the pattern of Cito contextsommen, a second model solves the Dutch text blind, a third checks grammar and naturalness, and the texts then enter a review queue. A native speaker should review them before use, which the parent records with the flag `nativeReviewed`, and no Dutch text is generated live. Presentations are separate streams, the Dutch ones stay out of «сама», and they feed the profile's dimension «Язык и формат». The report shows the presentations by node and overall, the Russian-to-Dutch gap and the Dutch-to-after-words gap with intervals, and the words after whose explanation she solved the task.

The events add `probe_family_created`, and `ItemShown` gains `purpose: "nl_probe"` and `probe?: { familyId; presentation: "bare" | "ru" | "nl" | "nl_after_words" | "cito_source" }`. Templates gain `probeFamily?`. Acceptance test 4 reads: presentations of one family share their difficulty features, never meet on one day, never change the «сама» estimate, and no text shows without its approval flag. Item 1 adds that every indicator shows its count and an 80 % interval, a thin one is marked «мало данных», and a reading is a hypothesis to check, never a diagnosis.

### Every Dutch sentence beyond the bridge's keywords breaks the Russian-only rule, which only the owner changes

`CLAUDE.md`, read 2026-09-28, says the text the player sees "is in Russian only for now, with one exception the owner added on 2026-09-28: the Dutch bridge's 30 to 50 keywords, kept as data beside the Russian text and approved by the parent word by word". ADR-0210 carries the same rule into the game. Every text and task she sees is Russian apart from the approved bridge keywords and the Dutch word a term hint shows. The scope guard fails on `content/i18n/nl.json` and on any `*.nl.*` file, and a group 1 check fails at the 51st bridge word. ADR-0160's string check refuses a Latin-script word on a player screen unless it is an approved Dutch field of the lexicon or a bridge keyword inside task content. RES-0800 defines the deferred Dutch layer as "Dutch task text, Dutch formats, templates with `curriculum: "nl"`", and the approved MVP scope, which ADR-0210 carries, lists the Dutch layer among the deferred items (RES-4080, read 2026-09-28).

The probe's presentations 3, 4 and 5 are whole Dutch task texts. They exceed the bridge's exception in kind, since they are full sentences and not keywords, and in number, since each text holds dozens of Dutch words. So the probe is part of the Dutch layer by RES-0800's definition, and it conflicts with `CLAUDE.md`, ADR-0210, ADR-0160, ADR-0190's scope guard and the approved MVP scope at once. `CLAUDE.md` is the repository's root policy and only the owner's direct instruction outranks it. An addendum item doesn't amend it, as the owner's separate change of 2026-09-28 for the bridge shows.

### "Cito's style" conflicts with ADR-0290's rule against a Cito layout, and Cito's items are closed

ADR-0290 says the game shows the player "no copy of a Cito item, no task laid out as one and no mock test", no template, string or data file may quote a Cito item, and a group 1 check fails on "Cito", "LVS" or "Leerling in beeld" in `src/templates/` or in any player string (read 2026-09-28). RES-4080 found that Cito calls practice on its items "geen goed idee", and that the Leerling in beeld items are closed, so nobody on the project could see one. A writer model told to follow "the pattern of Cito contextsommen" needs Cito items as its pattern, and those are either closed or, where a copy leaks, exactly what the rule forbids. The presentation value `cito_source` puts the word into the event schema, which the word check doesn't cover today but whose spirit it breaks. What the addendum needs is the genre: a short Dutch word problem of the kind a Dutch primary maths lesson uses, with context, numbers in a sentence and a question. A person can describe that genre in a style guide from the school's own lessons, and the game can write it without any Cito item.

### `PLANNER_MODEL` can't run offline, and the approved pipeline for model-written task text is ADR-0130's

ADR-0100 lists `PLANNER_MODEL` as a play role with its default model set by RES-1600, and its gateway refuses a play role on the offline key outside verify mode (read 2026-09-28). ADR-0210 requires every new model job to run under a role of its own, and it gave the offline framing and puzzle jobs their own roles, `FRAMING_MODEL` and `PUZZLE_MODEL`, "the model `PLANNER_MODEL` uses" as their default, on the content tier and the offline key. It uses `CHECK_MODEL` "as it stands" for the blind check of puzzles.

ADR-0130 already builds model-written task text in a way the probe can reuse. A model writes a frame with placeholders, never numbers. Code fills it with three sets of numbers from the template's own generator under three seeds, and a checking model solves each filled problem blind, seeing only the text. The frame passes only when all three answers match the engine's, and the parent then accepts, rejects or edits it in the Parent Room. That pipeline gives the probe "new numbers with the same difficulty features" for free, because the template's generator draws the numbers within the template's difficulty parameters. It also means no model ever sees her answers.

### The forbidden-word gate reads only Russian

ADR-0160 runs every line the heroine sees through `textGate` against one list, `content/shaming.ru.json` (read 2026-09-28). A Dutch sentence passes a Russian list untouched, so a Dutch text with a shaming or frightening word would reach her unchecked. The bridge's keywords escape this gap only because the parent approves each of them one by one.

### T problems reach play only through the Guardian, whose phases and button differ from a bare letter

RES-3900 concludes that word problems (T) "must reach play through the Guardians" (read 2026-09-28). RES-4040 and ADR-0250 add that every T1 to T4 problem opens with a choice of one model from four, alternates step input with final-answer input, and shows «Нельзя узнать» (can't be known) in every phase. A missing-data problem carries no bridge keyword, because a word she can't read looks like a missing given (ADR-0250). A probe family on T1 to T4 must show the same construction as a bare sum and as text. A Guardian problem with model choice and step input measures more than the bare presentation does, so the presentations would differ in form as well as in language. A T letter outside the Guardian breaks RES-3900's rule. ADR-0250 also gives every T1 to T4 problem exactly 4 "what's missing" options, the first naming the asked quantity in Russian, and it rejected a final-answer-only T problem because the missing phases leak which kind of problem it is. A letter that drops the phases, the button or the options breaks those rules. A letter that keeps them breaks the probe: the bare presentation can carry neither the button nor the options, Russian options would translate the question inside a Dutch presentation, and a press on a Dutch text she can't read would enter ADR-0250's refusal guard as a refusal.

### The twin after a wrong answer would put a second presentation on the same day

ADR-0080 gives one second attempt after a wrong first answer, on "a parallel task that ADR-0040's generator builds with the same template, subtype and difficulty features and new numbers" (read 2026-09-28). That twin is, by construction, another member of the probe family, shown on the same day. The addendum forbids two presentations of one family on one day, and a twin in Dutch after a wrong Dutch answer would also teach the words before the family's presentation 4.

### The bridge's share and the language-risk limit would count the letters

ADR-0210 as amended by ADR-0360 sets the bridge's keywords at 15 % to 25 % of the T1 to T4 and Sources tasks shown on nodes at «Понимает» (understands) or above, over any 14 game days (read 2026-09-28). A Dutch letter on a T or Sources template is such a task, and a Russian presentation that happened to carry bridge keywords would be a mixed task, not a Russian one. ADR-0180's language-risk limit counts mistakes on tasks with a risk term whose explanation she didn't open. A Dutch letter has risk terms in every sentence, so the limit would fill with the probe's own errors and stop telling the parent about Russian tasks.

### The Mainland isn't in the canon

CAN-0020, read 2026-09-28, sets the town of «Туманный Брод» (Misty Ford), the forest and the Tower, and names no land beyond them and no language other than the story's. «Большая земля» (the Mainland) and its letters are a new place and a new story device, and the canon changes only through its own records.

### Language weighs more on context problems than on bare sums, in Dutch pupils too

Hickendorff analysed over 2,000 pupils in grades 1 to 3 in the Netherlands with a multidimensional item response model. Computation and applied problem solving correlated at 0.81 in grade 1 to 0.87 in grade 3, "highly correlated but still distinct", and language level had a stronger effect on applied problem solving than on computation (Applied Measurement in Education, 2013, abstract read 2026-09-28 on ERIC). Greisen and colleagues found, in two cohorts of third graders in Luxembourg, that reading comprehension in German, the language of instruction, significantly mediated the lower maths results of pupils whose home language is far from it (Acta Psychologica, 2021, abstract read 2026-09-28). Both support the probe's premise: a pupil who learns maths in a second language can lose points on context problems that bare sums don't show. Neither measured one child, so neither says how large her gap is.

### Simplifying the language of a problem raises scores only a little

Abedi and Lord gave 1,174 eighth graders in Los Angeles 10 original NAEP items and 10 items with the same maths in simpler English, with the booklets randomised within classes. The revised items scored higher by 0.16 of 10 items, 2.9 %, statistically significant (t = 2.95, p = .003). The interaction between item type and learner status wasn't significant: second-language learners gained 3.7 % and fluent speakers 2.4 %. In interviews, 63 % and then 83 % of 36 pupils preferred the revised item. The authors cite a 10 % to 30 % drop on word problems against the same problems in numeric form (Applied Measurement in Education, 14(3), 2001, read 2026-09-28). The paper also lists the features they changed: unfamiliar words, passive verbs, long noun phrases, conditional and relative clauses, complex question phrases and impersonal presentations. That list is a usable checklist for how hard a Dutch text reads, apart from its words.

### A glossary closes 11 % to 21 % of a second-language gap and leaves maths scores of fluent readers unchanged

Kieffer, Rivera and Francis report their 2009 meta-analysis, updated to 20 studies with more than 33,000 pupils, most tested on NAEP maths and science items in grades 4 and 8. English dictionaries or glossaries had a small, significant effect, "equal to an 11 percent to 21 percent reduction in observed achievement gaps", with a mean effect size of 0.14 over 18 samples (Center on Instruction, 2012, read 2026-09-28). Pop-up glossaries on a computer worked even under time limits, by Pennock-Roman and Rivera's reading. "None of the accommodations was found to significantly improve the performance of non-ELLs", so a glossary doesn't change what the maths item measures. The authors conclude that "a much larger proportion of the math and science achievement differences" comes from "real differences in relevant academic language", not from language an accommodation can remove.

Two consequences follow for the probe. A word card in presentation 4 is a valid accommodation, since it leaves the maths intact. A Dutch gap that stays after the cards can come from academic Dutch, such as "hoeveel meer", "per" or "naar verhouding", which a card can't teach in one look. So a small recovery after the cards points to a language cause the cards can't reach, and says nothing yet about the maths.

### Parallel forms reduce the practice effect and don't remove it

Hausknecht and colleagues summarised 50 studies, 107 samples and 134,436 people retested on cognitive ability tests, and found an adjusted overall effect of 0.26. "Effects were larger when practice was accompanied by test coaching and when identical forms were used" (Journal of Applied Psychology, 2007, abstract read 2026-09-28). A probe family is a set of alternate forms with coaching in between, since a wrong answer brings a short solution. So a later presentation will score higher than an earlier one of the same family for reasons unrelated to its language. Balancing the order spreads that gain evenly across presentations, which is what the addendum asks for.

### The gap after the word cards includes practice and coaching that balancing can't remove

This finding is my reasoning from Hausknecht's result, not a measurement. Presentation 4 always follows presentation 3 of its family (the carryover finding below), so presentation 4 always sits at a later position and collects the practice gain of every presentation before it. When presentation 3 went wrong, she also read its short solution, which is coaching on the same construction. So the Dutch-to-after-words gap, and the list of words shown in presentation 4 of a family whose presentation 3 went wrong, both overstate what the words did. The balanced presentations, bare, Russian and source, measure the same practice gain directly: their accuracy by position in the family is the gain one more meeting brings, with the language held fixed. The report can show that gain beside the after-words gap, so the parent sees how much of the recovery practice alone would give.

### Alternating conditions in one child needs counterbalancing, and some carryover can't be balanced

Barlow and Hayes describe the alternating treatments design, which compares conditions within one subject. They divide its interference into "sequential confounding, carryover effects, and alternation effects", and control them by rapid alternation and counterbalancing (Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis, 1979, abstract read 2026-09-28). The probe is such a design with five conditions and one pupil. The word cards cause a carryover counterbalancing can't undo. Once she has read the cards for a text, a later presentation 3 of the same family is no longer a Dutch text without help. Across families the same carryover is slow and wanted: the Dutch words she learns from cards make later presentations 3 easier, which the report will show as a gap narrowing over weeks.

### Tapping a word inside presentation 3 and moving the attempt to presentation 4 biases both cells

This finding is my reasoning, not a source. If a tap moves an attempt out of presentation 3, then presentation 3 keeps only the attempts where she felt no need for a word. Those are the texts she read well, so presentation 3's accuracy rises and the Russian-to-Dutch gap shrinks. Presentation 4 then mixes two conditions: planned explanations, where the cards came first and the numbers are new, and her own taps in the middle of a text. A tap in the middle is help she asked for, in the same sense as a hint rung, and the approved model already takes only a graded, unassisted first attempt as an observation of «сама» (ADR-0060).

### A text a model wrote can make the language gap look larger than it is

This finding is my reasoning, not a source. A blind solve by a model checks that the text's maths has one answer; it doesn't check that the Dutch reads like a Dutch primary lesson. A model's Dutch that is stiff, too formal or wrong in a preposition makes presentation 3 harder for reasons that neither the school's Dutch nor the maths causes. That error runs in one direction only, towards a language problem, which is the direction the owner's falsifiability rule guards against most. The model grammar check narrows it, and only a native reader closes it. The owner's addendum calls native review desirable and not required.

### The probe's volume gives about 20 observations a presentation overall and about 3 a node

At about 4 probe tasks a game day for 21 to 28 game days, the probe shows 84 to 112 tasks. The addendum's groups are T1 to T4, P, F, M and the Sources track, 8 groups at 1 to 2 templates each, so 8 to 16 templates. A family on a maths node has 5 presentations, or 4 without a natural source, and a family on a Sources template has no bare presentation. So the first phase makes about 18 to 24 families. The Russian, Dutch and Dutch-after-words presentations appear in every family, so each gets about 18 to 24 observations. The bare presentation is missing from the 1 to 2 Sources track families in every 8 to 16, so it gets about 16 to 21. The source presentation appears only on the maths templates where a table or chart suits the node, which nobody has counted yet, so it will probably stay under 12 in the first phase. These figures are my arithmetic from the addendum's numbers, not a measurement.

With 20 observations, the 80 % Wilson interval around 13 right answers is 51 % to 77 %, and around 18 right is 78 % to 96 %. The addendum's own example, 91 % Russian against 63 % Dutch, gives an 80 % interval for the gap of about 12 to 44 points at 20 observations each, so the gap shows. A 10-point gap, 91 % against 81 %, gives about -4 to 24 points, so the probe can't tell it from zero within the first phase. A node has 2 to 3 families, so each per-node cell holds 2 to 3 observations, where 2 right of 3 spans 32 % to 89 %. The owner's example hypothesis in item 8 names a gap "over 20 points", which the first phase can just resolve.

### Nothing from item 6 needs to join the MVP's log

The log already records, for every task shown, its full rendered view with its `locale`, template and template version, seed, parameters, node, subtype, purpose, attempt number and `forms` (SPC-0020, read 2026-09-28). Each tap on a bridge card writes `bridge_card_opened` (ADR-0210). The probe's families are built when the probe starts, from templates and seeds, and its comparison needs only presentations shown inside a family. So no probe field has to be written from the first day for a later report to work, and the new fields can arrive with the probe in a later payload version, as ADR-0210 allows.

### Four options, and each wins at something

| Option | What its advocate says it is better at | The case against it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Do nothing: keep the bridge and ADR-0290's bare-versus-context split | No Dutch text, no amendment of the Russian-only rule, no new stream; the split already compares bare sums with Russian word problems on every node | It never shows her a Dutch problem, so it can't separate language from format, and it leaves the owner's main practical question unanswered |
| B. The probe as the addendum writes it, in the MVP | Measures before the M7 horizon of 2027-01-15, when the owner most wants the answer | Breaks the Russian-only rule and the MVP scope without the owner's amendment, and its tap rule and "Cito style" bias the gap towards a language cause |
| C. The probe after the MVP, once the owner amends the Russian-only rule, with the fixes below | Keeps every approved rule until the owner changes one, and reads the gap without the known biases | Its answer may arrive after the M7 test, and it adds a stream, three offline roles, a text queue and a Dutch forbidden list |
| D. Dutch texts written by a native speaker only, no model | Natural Dutch from the start, so the bias from a model's text disappears | About 60 Dutch texts for the first phase, 18 to 24 families at 3 Dutch presentations each, is many hours for a tutor, and ADR-0130 already chose model drafts under review for exactly that cost |

Research leads with option C, because it is the only option that answers the owner's question and keeps the root policy intact. The strongest case against it is timing: the probe's first phase needs 3 to 4 weeks after the MVP, and if the MVP lands in December, the answer comes after the M7 test it was meant to explain. That case doesn't change the choice, because the probe measures a lasting question, not a date. The E7 horizon of 2027-05-15 and the next year's tests still profit from it, and option B would buy an earlier answer with a biased gap. The evidence gives no reason to abandon the idea: Hickendorff's Dutch data and the accommodation meta-analysis both say the language part of a context problem is real in pupils like her. Option D survives as a choice for the parent inside C, since `nativeReviewed` lets a tutor review what the model drafts.

## Conclusions

1. The owner must amend the Russian-only rule in `CLAUDE.md` before any probe text in Dutch is built, because the probe's Dutch texts exceed the bridge's exception in kind and in number. The same amendment must reach ADR-0210's rule on player text, ADR-0160's string check, ADR-0190's scope guard and the approved MVP scope through new records that name what they replace.
2. The probe must ship after the MVP, as part of the Dutch layer, behind a parent switch `probe.enabled` that is off by default. When the switch is off, no letter, no Dutch probe text and no probe card may appear anywhere in the game. The switch starts off because the probe can't run before the parent has approved enough texts to fill a family, and because Dutch letters are a choice the parent makes for her, as the bridge's switch already is.
3. Every probe task must come from an ordinary template tied to a node, and every presentation of a family must draw its numbers from that template's generator under a new seed with the same difficulty parameters. Presentations of one family must differ only in their presentation, because any other difference would enter the gap beside the language.
4. No probe text, prompt, template or data file may quote or imitate a Cito item, and the writer model must receive no Cito item. The Dutch texts must follow the genre of a Dutch primary word problem as a person describes it in a style guide. The presentation value must be `nl_source`, never a name with "Cito" in it.
5. Dutch probe texts must be written offline as frames with placeholders under a new offline role, `PROBE_TEXT_MODEL`, on the content tier and the offline key. Each frame must pass a blind solve by `CHECK_MODEL` on three seeds, with the checking model seeing only the filled text. It must also pass a grammar and naturalness check under a second new offline role, `PROBE_LANGUAGE_MODEL`, and then the parent's review. The three roles must use three different model ids, and a probe run must refuse to start otherwise, because a checker on the writer's own model shares the writer's blind spots. No probe text may be generated during play, and `PLANNER_MODEL` must not write probe text.
6. The offline run that writes probe texts must receive no data about the player: no answer, no tap and no word she opened, because only content made without the player may leave the Mac.
7. Every Dutch probe text must pass a Dutch forbidden-word list before it enters the parent's queue, because `textGate` reads only Russian.
8. Presentation 4 of a family must follow its presentation 3 on a later game day. The positions of the bare, Russian and source presentations must be drawn from the adventure's seeded stream so that each comes before and after the Dutch pair equally often across families.
9. No two presentations of one family may appear on one game day, and all presentations of a family must fall within 14 days of its first presentation, whether she plays on them or not. The approved model treats checks less than 14 days apart as one window, since «Устойчиво» (stable) needs two checks at least 14 days apart (ADR-0060). A family inside that window meets one state of her skill, and I chose the same number and the same count of plain days for that reason. A family she can't finish inside the window closes with the presentations it has, and the report counts them.
10. A tap on a word card during presentation 3 must count that attempt as not unassisted, like a hint rung, and the attempt must stay in presentation 3's count. The log must record the opened words on the attempt. The report must show beside presentation 3 how many attempts opened cards and how many of those were right.

### Decided on 2026-09-28

The probe is built after the MVP, behind `probe.enabled`, and only once the owner has amended the Russian-only rule in `CLAUDE.md`. Its Dutch texts are Dutch task text, which RES-0800 places in the deferred Dutch layer and `CLAUDE.md` forbids beyond the bridge's keywords, and only the owner changes `CLAUDE.md`. Nothing from item 6 joins the MVP's log, because the log already records what the probe will need. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

The Dutch texts follow the genre of a Dutch primary word problem from a person's style guide, the writer sees no Cito item, and the presentation is named `nl_source`. ADR-0290 forbids a task laid out as a Cito item, Cito advises against practice on its items, and its items are closed. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

The texts are written offline under `PROBE_TEXT_MODEL`, solved blind by `CHECK_MODEL` on three seeds, and checked under `PROBE_LANGUAGE_MODEL`. The three roles must use three different model ids, and a probe run refuses to start otherwise. `PLANNER_MODEL` is a play role the gateway refuses on the offline key, ADR-0210 gives every new job a role of its own, and a checker on the writer's own model shares the writer's blind spots. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

Presentation 5 is shown in Dutch through ADR-0300's `SourceView`. A family on a Sources track template has three presentations: a Russian source, a Dutch source and a Dutch source after the words. The addendum lists the source among the Dutch presentations, and the Sources track already supplies a Russian source stream for comparison. A bare presentation has no meaning for a task whose content is a source. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

Presentation 4 always follows presentation 3 on a later game day, and the other presentations take balanced positions around that pair. A card read before presentation 3 would teach its words, a carryover that counterbalancing can't undo. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

A tap on a card during presentation 3 keeps the attempt in presentation 3, counted as not unassisted, and the report shows the taps beside it. This departs from the addendum's rule that a tap moves the attempt to presentation 4, because that rule leaves presentation 3 with only the texts she read well and mixes two conditions in presentation 4. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

`nativeReviewed` stays optional, as the owner wrote, and every text still needs the parent's approval before it shows. The report marks a gap whose Dutch observations come less than half from native-reviewed texts, because a model's unnatural Dutch biases the gap towards a language cause. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

The first phase ends when the Russian, Dutch and Dutch-after-words presentations each hold 20 observations, or after 28 game days on which she plays. A cell reads «мало данных» below 12 observations. At 20 the probe resolves a gap of about 28 points and just reaches the owner's 20-point example. The threshold of 12 is a judgement: at 12 observations and one half right, the 80 % interval spans about 33 % to 67 %, 34 points wide, and I judge a wider interval too loose to show the parent as a number. Counting game days with play keeps the rule free of any clock. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

Every presentation arrives as a letter in one slot, a fixed part of the floor like the Sources track's tasks, with at most 2 letters on one floor, and pays the ordinary reward of its kind. A frame or a reward that differed by presentation would become a second difference beside the language. A fixed part keeps the Director's room slots and the flow corridor as they stand, the same way ADR-0300 placed the Sources track's tasks. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

A T letter takes a final-answer input in every presentation, shows neither «Нельзя узнать» nor the four options, and never uses a surplus or unanswerable subtype. The bare presentation can carry no model choice, steps, button or options, so the text presentations must not carry them either, or the presentations would differ in more than language. ADR-0250's button hides which kind a problem is, and a letter is never unanswerable, so leaving it out hides nothing that exists. Keeping it would put Russian options into Dutch presentations and let a Dutch word she can't read enter the refusal guard. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

A probe letter gets no twin, and a wrong answer brings the short solution only. The approved twin is built from the same template, subtype and difficulty features with new numbers, so any twin, Russian or Dutch, is a second presentation of the family on the same day, and it adds practice only to the families she got wrong. A twin from another template would still teach the construction the family measures. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

Every presentation, bare and Russian included, writes `nl_probe` and stays out of «сама», the language-risk limit and the bridge's share. A letter is a new form: it arrives outside the Director's slots, and a T letter drops the Guardian's model choice and steps, so ADR-0210 keeps it out of «сама» until a refit admits it. Letters would otherwise swamp the language-risk limit meant for Russian tasks, and push the bridge's measured share out of its band. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.
