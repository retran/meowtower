# Addendum 2's falsifiability holds only if every figure carries its count and 80 % interval, a hypothesis is judged on data logged after it, and check 5 adds a student with no gap; only the parts that shape the log join the MVP

## Summary

The owner's addendum 2 of 2026-09-28 restates the project's goal as a profile of her maths and the real boundary of what she does on her own, and makes falsifiability a required property of the report. The approved report already keeps most of what that needs: it is computed from the log, it shows «мало данных» (too little data) for thin limits, and it words the language cause as «возможна» (possible). Four things break the property as the record stands. The parent's exclusion of an ambiguous task can remove wrong answers with no trace in the report. The summary lists nodes where language may explain the errors and none where it can't. Uncertainty is an entropy number and not an interval over counts the parent can check. And with eight dimensions at 80 % intervals, at least one interval misses its true value about 83 % of the time, so a report that reads every interval as a finding manufactures strengths and weaknesses. Build check 5 as written can pass on a report that sees a gap everywhere, because it has no synthetic student without a gap.

I decide below that shares get the 80 % Wilson interval over raw counts, as RES-4210 decides for the profile, and that each measure owns its «мало данных» floor, with 5 observations as the default. A hypothesis's status reads only events logged after it, and check 5 runs four synthetic students over 20 seeds. The four states the school mixes map onto three approved labels and one profile reading. The MVP gains only what a later report can't rebuild: recording hypotheses, the transfer record's context tags, hold and first encounters, `itemId` on `solution_shown` and `hint_shown`, and an optional `categories` field on the Cito form. The rest goes to the backlog. The Dutch probe needs the owner to amend the Russian-only rule in `CLAUDE.md` and an offline role for its texts. This record covers item 1, the event list and field changes, the report additions, the build checks and scope. Items 2 to 8 belong to their own records.

## The question

What must the approved record now say so that the report can show a weak side as clearly as a strong one, and which of the addendum's new events, fields, report parts and checks must exist from the first day of play?

The addendum assumes that falsifiability is a property of how the report displays results: show everything, show uncertainty, word interpretations as hypotheses. That assumption is only half right. A report can be complete and honest in its display and still be unfalsifiable, in three ways. First, if the analysis is chosen after the data is seen, any pattern can be explained, which is Gelman and Loken's point about forking paths. Second, many 80 % intervals shown side by side produce chance "findings" at a known rate. Third, the tasks themselves decide what can be seen: a language cause can be ruled out only when the game logs attempts in which language can't be the cause. So the question is also about when a hypothesis is recorded, how many comparisons the report draws, and what the log holds. The addendum's own sentence that the school "seems to" mix four states is itself a hypothesis of this kind, and the game should be able to refute it.

The node card shows the «сама» estimate "with its uncertainty" (ADR-0180 line 49). ### For small counts, the Wilson or the equal-tailed Jeffreys interval is the recommended interval for a share

Brown, Cai and DasGupta show that the textbook Wald interval's coverage is erratic far more persistently than assumed. They recommend "the Wilson interval or the equal-tailed Jeffreys prior interval for small n and the interval suggested in Agresti and Coull for larger n". Both intervals need only the count of right answers and the count of attempts. The Wilson interval is closed-form, and the Jeffreys interval needs the inverse of the incomplete beta function, as RES-4210 notes. Source: Brown, Cai and DasGupta, abstract, read 2026-09-28; RES-4210, read 2026-09-28.

### At her volumes an 80 % interval is wide, and a node's probe cell stays «мало данных»

I computed 80 % Wilson intervals, and the equal-tailed Jeffreys intervals agree with them within 0.01. At 3 of 5 right, the interval runs from 0.33 to 0.82, about 50 points wide. At 5 of 10 it runs from 0.31 to 0.69, and at 10 of 20 from 0.36 to 0.64. At 20 of 40 it is still 20 points wide. For a difference between two shares with 20 attempts each, the normal approximation gives an 80 % half-width of about 0.16 at 90 % against 60 %, and about 0.20 at 50 % against 50 %. Item 6 plans about 4 probe tasks a day for 3 to 4 weeks, 84 to 112 tasks over five presentations and eight node groups. That is about 17 to 22 tasks a presentation pooled, and 2 to 3 a presentation per node group. So a pooled gap of about 20 points, the size item 8's example hypothesis names, is borderline at these volumes, and per-node rows can't resolve any gap. RES-4250 ends the probe's first phase when the Russian, Dutch and Dutch-after-words presentations each hold 20 observations. I simulated that volume with 40,000 draws, with Newcombe's hybrid score interval at 95 % on each language contrast. A language gap of 45 points (Russian at 90 %, Dutch at 45 %, Dutch after the words at 85 %) gives a language line on about 95 % of draws, and no gap (every presentation at 90 %) on about 1.5 %. A maths gap, every presentation at 55 %, gives a language line on about 4 % of draws, and its pooled bare and Russian share has a 95 % Wilson upper bound below 0.8 on about 96 %. Both gaps together (Russian at 55 %, Dutch at 15 %, Dutch after the words at 50 %) give the language line on about 88 % and the maths line on about 96 %. A gap of 35 points gives the language line on only about 81 %. Source: my computation with `scipy.stats` and `numpy`, run 2026-09-28; RES-4250 conclusion 19, read 2026-09-28; the addendum, items 6 and 8, read 2026-09-28.

### Many 80 % intervals side by side produce chance findings at a known rate

An 80 % interval misses its true value 1 time in 5, half of those on each side. Across the profile's eight dimensions, the chance that at least one interval misses is 1 - 0.8^8, about 83 %. Across 40 node-level comparisons, about 8 would exclude the true value by chance. Gelman and Loken show that researcher degrees of freedom create a multiple-comparisons problem even when only one analysis is run. The reason is that the analysis would have been chosen differently had the data been different. A report that turns any interval clearing a threshold into «что проверить» (what to check) is that case. Source: my computation, 2026-09-28; Gelman and Loken, abstract and section 1, read 2026-09-28.

### A hypothesis is tested only by observations made after it was stated

Nosek and colleagues separate generating a hypothesis from existing observations and testing it with new ones, and they call the confusion of postdiction with prediction a loss of credibility. Their remedy is to define the question and the analysis plan before the outcomes are observed. Item 8 asks the parent to record a hypothesis before looking at the data, with what would confirm and what would refute it. The addendum doesn't say which observations judge it. A hypothesis recorded today about the language gap and judged on last month's word problems is a postdiction dressed as a test, even if the parent never opened the report. Source: Nosek, Ebersole, DeHaven and Mellor, abstract, read 2026-09-28; the addendum, items 1 and 8, read 2026-09-28.

### Word problems score below bare sums in the pupil's own language, so the language contrast is Russian against Dutch

Abedi and Lord gave 1,174 pupils in the 8th grade released NAEP (National Assessment of Educational Progress) maths items and parallel items with simplified language. Pupils learning English scored lower, and the linguistically modified items scored slightly higher, most of all for pupils in low and average maths classes. The same paper cites Carpenter and colleagues (1980) for children doing 10 % to 30 % worse on word problems than on the same problems in numeric form. I didn't read Carpenter, so that range is unverified. The addendum's check 5 defines the maths-gap student by weak bare and weak Russian tasks. A gap between bare and Russian tasks is expected in any child, so it can't separate maths from language. The separating contrasts are Russian against Dutch, and Dutch against Dutch after the words are explained. Source: Abedi and Lord, pages 219 to 221, read 2026-09-28; the addendum, items 6 and check 5, read 2026-09-28.

### Graduated help and transfer are an established way to read "knows with a little help" and "beyond the format"

Campione and Brown's dynamic assessment gives graduated help and then measures transfer to new situations. In their first two studies, children of different abilities differed in learning and in transfer, the differences were greater in transfer, and transfer predicted performance better than IQ or standardised scores. Their introduction says a static unaided score is often read as a fixed ability and can underestimate a child from a different cultural background. That is the addendum's claim about the school, in the general case. The approved hint ladder (ADR-0220) and the addendum's transfer measure (item 4) follow this design. Source: Campione and Brown, ED269735, abstract and pages 3 and 4, read 2026-09-28.

### Check 5 can pass on a report that always finds a gap, and its students come from the same author as the code

The addendum's check 5 runs two synthetic students, one with a maths gap and one with a language gap, and expects the profile to point each to its own side. A report that shows a gap in both dimensions for every student passes half of each case, and a report biased towards language fails only the first. Neither student tests what falsifiability needs most: that a student with no gap gets no finding. ADR-0190's ten profiles in RES-2900 model node knowledge only, and none varies with the presentation, so none can play either student. ADR-0190's strongest objection applies in full: the same agent writes the synthetic students and the code, so a green check shows the code agrees with its own assumptions. Wilson and Collins give the remedy for models fitted to behaviour under the rule "Check that you can recover your parameters": "First, simulate fake data with known parameter values. Next, fit the model to these fake data to try to 'recover' the parameters. Finally, compare the recovered parameters to their true values." Check 5 is that test for the profile. It shows the report can tell apart what the generator encodes, and it can't show what her gap is. Source: the addendum, check 5, read 2026-09-28; ADR-0190 and RES-2900, read 2026-09-28; Wilson and Collins, abstract, rule headings and the parameter-recovery steps, read 2026-09-28.

### Decided on 2026-09-28

A share in the report carries its count and the 80 % Wilson interval over raw, unweighted counts in the figure's stated window. A difference between two shares carries Newcombe's hybrid score interval, built from the two Wilson intervals, and a median time carries the distribution-free 80 % interval from order statistics. RES-4210 decides the same family for the profile, and one family across the report keeps two screens from disagreeing on one figure. Raw counts let the parent recompute each figure from the attempts the card lists. Wilson is one of the two intervals Brown and colleagues recommend at small counts, and its closed form gives the same numbers on every recompute, which check 3 needs, where Jeffreys needs an inverse incomplete beta. ADR-0060's entropy uncertainty stays for the Director. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

Each measure owns its «мало данных» floor, and a figure below it shows «мало данных» with its count in place of its value and enters no interpretation, quadrant, profile line or hypothesis status. A profile bar reads «мало данных» under 10 observations or when its 80 % interval is wider than 30 points (RES-4210). A transfer figure does so under 10 eligible observations (RES-4230), a probe cell under 12 (RES-4250), and a hypothesis condition can't be met under 20 (RES-4270). Any other share uses 5, the floor ADR-0240 and ADR-0300 already use for a cell. Each floor sits with the measure because the measures differ in what they compare and how fast their data comes, and a condition that turns a figure into a verdict needs more than a figure that is only shown. The approved floors for limits and for the interest signal stay, because they count sessions and days. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

An interpretation the report draws on its own is worded as «что проверить» (what to check) and names a check the game or the parent can run: a probe, a retention check, the sandbox or a question to the teacher. It never names a lesson, a date or an exercise, because ADR-0180 forbids a lesson plan. The report draws such lines only on a fixed list of pooled contrasts that the report's code declares before any data exists. The language line reads Russian against Dutch and Dutch against Dutch after the words. The maths line reads the pooled bare and Russian share against 0.8, the block score of 4 in 5 that ADR-0060 asks for «Бегло». The profile's own change lines follow RES-4210. A line appears only when the 95 % interval of the difference excludes zero, or the 95 % Wilson interval of a level lies wholly above or below its threshold. Otherwise the line reads «пока не ясно» (not clear yet). Per-node figures show their 80 % intervals and draw no line. The fixed list removes the forking paths Gelman and Loken describe, and the 95 % trigger cuts the chance rate per contrast from about 20 % to about 5 %, while the addendum's 80 % stays the interval the parent reads.
