# A first encounter measures transfer only if the Director holds it until the subtype is fluent, and the record must tag each task's context from the first day, because frames spend every new context within weeks

## Summary

The owner's addendum 2 of 2026-09-28, item 4, asks the game to record her first unassisted attempt on a new subtype, format or context as a transfer observation, near for a new format or context and far for a new subtype. It asks the Director to keep some first encounters in reserve, and it adds `contexts` and `formats` to templates and `firstExposure` to the show event, which this record computes in a projection. The approved record already has a `format` field, one of `bare` or `context` per template, but it has no notion of a task's context. Its frame rule shows never-shown frames first, so without a change every new context is spent in training long before the subtype is mastered. The addendum's reserve lasts "the first 2 weeks", a calendar span that has nothing to do with mastery, so most near observations would still fall on subtypes she hasn't learned. The literature agrees that near and far have no fixed meaning and that a hint turns a transfer test into an execution test. It also finds that high variability helps learners who know a domain and hurts novices, so holding variety back until a subtype is fluent costs little learning. Research decided the open questions on 2026-09-28. The format axis stays ADR-0290's `format` and a context becomes a tag from a closed list on every frame. The reserve holds the context format of each subtype until its node is fluent by a tested result or for at most 14 game days, and its last unshown context until its node is fluent, with no cap. Only an eligible graded first attempt counts, a hint before the answer counts as not transferred, and the reserve, the tags and `firstExposure` join the MVP while the report section waits for the backlog. I cover item 4 and its acceptance test 2, and leave the profile, retention and the Dutch probe to the records on items 2, 3 and 6.

## The question

What must now be true of the game, its data and the approved record so that a first encounter measures transfer, and so that first encounters still exist when she has learned a subtype? The owner's addendum is the instruction, dated 2026-09-28, and it outranks the approved record where they disagree.

The addendum makes four assumptions, and I challenge each before comparing options. First, it assumes a first attempt in the game is a first encounter for her. It isn't: she learns maths at school every week, so the log can tell only what is new in the game, and a far observation may be a subtype her class practised the day before. Second, it assumes "near" and "far" name a known scale. The transfer literature uses both words for very different distances, as the findings show, so the report has to say exactly what each word counts. Third, it assumes a reserve kept for "the first weeks" leaves fresh variants for subtypes she has mastered. Mastery doesn't follow the calendar: a node she meets about once a week may take a month to reach «бегло» (fluent), so a two-week reserve releases most variants while she is still learning. Fourth, it assumes formats and contexts are things a template can list. The record has formats, one per template, but no context anywhere, and the text of a word problem comes from a frame chosen apart from the template.

## Findings

### The addendum sets three kinds of first encounter, two distances, a reserve and three data changes

The owner's addendum, item 4, read 2026-09-28, defines a transfer observation as the first attempt in her history, made without help, on something new. The new thing is one of three kinds. The first is a subtype of a node whose prerequisites are all «бегло» or above. The second is a format of the same subtype, with the examples bare expression to word problem, text to source, and direct problem to inverse. The third is a context, a new situation in a problem of the same type, such as motion by boat where she has met motion on foot. Near transfer is a new context or format of the same subtype, far transfer a new subtype or node, and the report shows them apart. The Director keeps first encounters in reserve: in the first weeks it doesn't spend all of a node's formats and contexts in training. Each template gets `contexts` and `formats`, a derived view marks the used ones, and the show event gets `firstExposure: { kind: "subtype" | "format" | "context"; distance: "near" | "far" }`. Acceptance test 2 reads: `firstExposure` is set exactly once per subtype, format and context, and the Director doesn't spend all of a node's formats in the first 2 weeks. Item 2 feeds the profile's transfer dimension from these observations, and item 1 requires each figure to carry its count and an 80 % interval.

### The record already has one `format` per template, so a `formats` list would duplicate it

ADR-0290 makes every template declare `format: "bare"` or `format: "context"`, and its template schema refuses a template without it. An approved requirement carries that rule, and `item_shown` records `format` (ADR-0290, SPC-0290, read 2026-09-28). In a node with both formats, the item builder takes a bare template whenever the node's bare scored tasks of the last 30 days number no more than its context ones. ADR-0360 item 65 moves that choice to the subtype, since the format follows the template. The report shows accuracy and fluency by format. A second field `formats` holding a list would give a template two declarations of one fact, and the half-bare rule, its simulation and the report all read the singular field.

### The addendum's other two formats already have homes outside a subtype

The approved graph makes direct and inverse problems separate subtypes or nodes. RES-0800 has A4 «Деление как обратное умножению» (division as the inverse of multiplication) as a node of its own and P7 for reverse percentages. RES-1200 lists "unknown component" tasks under A11 and A15, and ADR-0050 lists "a side from the perimeter" as its own M4 subtype (all read 2026-09-28). A first attempt on an inverse problem is therefore already a first attempt on a new subtype, which the addendum counts as far.

The Sources track, ADR-0300, keeps tables, charts, timetables and maps in five track nodes, I1 to I5, apart from the 79 maths nodes, with no prerequisites, no probes and no inference (read 2026-09-28). A source task is a task of a track subtype, never a format of a maths subtype. The addendum's subtype rule needs every prerequisite at «бегло», and a track node has none, so the "text to source" format can't produce an eligible observation.

### The record has no notion of a task's context

ADR-0130 sends the text of every word problem and context task through a frame: text with placeholders, written by a model for a structure, checked, and accepted by the parent. Each frame records its structure, locale, floor, characters and the role of each number, and `item_shown` carries `frameId`, `frameSource` and `frameRepeat` (read 2026-09-28). Neither the frame nor the template nor the event names the situation a problem is set in. The situation lives only in the frame's words, so today the log can't say whether a problem about boats is her first one. A context tag added to frames later would have to be assigned by reading every accepted frame again. A bare task has no frame and no context. The `frame_accepted` event holds each frame's text and hash, so a tag it also holds lets the log give every show's context through `frameId`, with no new field on `item_shown`.

### The frame rule spends never-shown frames first, so contexts run out while she is learning

ADR-0130 picks, for each task, the least recently shown frame of its structure and locale, never-shown frames first, and two approved requirements forbid a repeat within 14 game days while the structure has an unshown frame (read 2026-09-28). The library holds at least 5 frames a structure by stage 0.2 and 20 to 30 by stage 0.4. Never-shown first is the right rule against repetition, and it is also a rule that shows every new frame, and so every context, as early as it can. A subtype she meets on 5 to 10 tasks before her node reaches «бегло» will have used most of its structure's contexts by then, and the one left, if any, comes by chance.

### Holding back the context format never breaks the half-bare rule, but holding back bare would

The half-bare rule asks that at least half of a node's scored tasks in 30 days be bare (ADR-0290). A subtype that shows only bare tasks while its context format is held raises the node's bare share, which the rule allows. A subtype held to context tasks, with bare kept back, lowers the bare share and can break the rule on a node whose other subtypes have no bare template. So only the context format can be held without a conflict, and the bare-to-context direction is also the addendum's own first example.

### Far transfer, as the addendum defines it, is the event the approved model already tests by inference

ADR-0060 infers a state for a node from its prerequisites, and ADR-0070 tests those inferences. An «понимает» (understands) state owes each direct descendant a probe, and every adventure day holds 1 or 2 island checks, each a probe of a node in "fluent (inferred)" (read 2026-09-28). The first probe of a node she has never met, whose prerequisites she has shown in the game, is exactly the addendum's far observation, so far observations arise from approved rules without any reserve. A stretch node follows the same pattern, since the approved stretch rule admits one only when each prerequisite is fluent or stable by a tested result, a probe or a full block. During cold start, the first 10 adventures at most, the Director probes each domain from group 7 or 8 downwards, and her prerequisites are still untested. So first encounters in cold start measure placement, not transfer.

### Several slots spend a first encounter without giving a graded, unassisted first attempt

ADR-0060 drops ungraded attempts, the warm-up among them, from every estimate. ADR-0220 gives a twin, a second attempt on a parallel task of the same template and subtype, after an `alt` outcome or a rung 2 or 3 hint. ADR-0070 drops a rapid guess from every estimate and gives the rest of a session weight 0.5 once fatigue fires (all read 2026-09-28). ADR-0210 sends every task with a non-empty `forms` list, such as a bridge task with Dutch keywords or an unanswerable T problem, into a stream of its own. Item 3 of addendum 2 adds a retention check and item 6 adds Dutch probe presentations (read 2026-09-28). ADR-0130 lets a live frame, checked by one more blind solve but never seen by the parent, serve a block top-up slot. A new context shown first in any of these places is used up without a clean observation. A new context in a Dutch task or a retention check would also mix transfer into another measurement.

### A lesson from the parent turns a first encounter into a taught one

ADR-0180 records lesson marks, the parent's note that she taught a node, and labels a later rise «Улучшилось после урока» (improved after a lesson) (read 2026-09-28). Addendum 2 item 3 drops an observation from retention when a lesson mark or a walkthrough of a prerequisite that gives the answer away falls in the 21 days before it. A first encounter after a lesson on the same node measures the lesson, by the same reasoning.

### A transfer view reads ordinary attempts, so ADR-0210's rule doesn't take them out of "on her own"

ADR-0210 drops an attempt from "on her own" only when its task's `forms` holds a form the active model doesn't admit, and fills `forms` from the template and the Director's choices (read 2026-09-28). A task in a new context or format is an ordinary task of its node with an empty `forms`, and it already counts in "on her own" today. A view that reads these attempts and marks some as first encounters adds no form. So it leaves the attempts where they are, and the view itself, like every stream, feeds no estimate. Taking first encounters out of the estimate would also hide the case the owner's falsifiability rule wants seen, a node that looks fluent and fails in a new setting.

### Near and far have no agreed meaning, and the addendum's labels differ from the nearest study of word problems

Barnett and Ceci review a century of transfer research and conclude that the terms "near transfer and far transfer have been used to mean very different things by different researchers". They replace the single scale with nine dimensions: three of content (the learned skill, the performance change, the memory demands) and six of context (knowledge domain, physical, temporal, functional and social context, and modality) (Psychological Bulletin 128(4), 612 to 637, 2002, read 2026-09-28). Every observation item 4 describes stays inside one knowledge domain, school maths, which their scheme would place near.

Fuchs and Fuchs describe the measures of their third-grade studies. On the immediate-transfer measure "the only source of novelty was the cover story". The near-transfer measure added "a novel superficial feature", one of a novel look, unfamiliar vocabulary, a different question or a larger problem. The far-transfer measure combined all of these with irrelevant information and new content (Journal of Special Education 39(1), 45 to 57, 2005, read 2026-09-28). In their scheme a new context alone is immediate transfer and a new format is near, and every problem type on every measure had been taught. The addendum's far kind, a subtype never taught in the game, is a different thing: generalising to untaught content from known prerequisites. The report therefore needs its own plain definition of each word.

### A hint or a prompt turns a transfer test into an execution test

Barnett and Ceci name memory demands as a content dimension: a test that hints which approach applies asks the learner only to execute, while one without hints asks her also to recognise when the approach applies. They warn that "the use of hints" changes the test "from recall to mere execution or recognition" (2002, read 2026-09-28). The first rung of ADR-0220's ladder shows a hint before she answers, so a first attempt that opened the ladder has lost the recognition part of the test.

### High variability helps learners who know a domain and hurts novices

Likourezos, Kalyuga and Sweller tested 56 adults in a pre-university mathematics programme: "More experienced learners learned more from high rather than low variability tasks", "while less experienced learners learned more from low rather than high variability tasks". They advise that "learners should initially be presented with low variability or easier tasks", with variability rising as experience grows (Educational Psychology Review 31, 479 to 497, 2019, ERIC abstract, read 2026-09-28). The sample was adults, so the result applies to her only by analogy. It still suggests that holding contexts and formats back until a subtype is fluent costs her little learning, and it gives no support to a hold that outlasts mastery.

### Learning from practice transfers most across test formats

Pan and Rickard's meta-analysis of 192 transfer effect sizes from 122 experiments found that practice tests yield learning that transfers, d = 0.40, 95 % CI [0.31, 0.50]. It also found that "transfer of learning is greatest across test formats", while intercepts corrected for publication bias often showed "no positive transfer" when none of the helping moderators was present (Psychological Bulletin 144(7), 710 to 756, 2018, read 2026-09-28). The studies were mostly of adults learning verbal material. For the report, the finding means a drop on the first context task of a fluent subtype is a real signal, since format is where transfer is usually easiest.

### She will produce few eligible observations, so only pooled figures carry meaning

ADR-0050's first graph holds 79 nodes and about 200 subtypes, and ADR-0070 plans about 30 graded first attempts an adventure day (read 2026-09-28). Each subtype gives at most one far observation. With the rules this record proposes, it gives at most two near ones: the first task in the context format and the last held context. Only those whose node or prerequisites are fluent by a tested result count. Cold start lowers the far count further. It lasts until fewer than half of the 1F and 1S nodes remain unchecked or the 10th adventure ends (ADR-0070), so it may show up to about half the nodes while their prerequisites are untested. Those first shows are ineligible and used up, so far observations come mainly from the nodes cold start leaves unshown. I estimate, and haven't measured, a few eligible observations a week and at most one or two per subtype over a school year, fewer for the nodes cold start reaches. A per-node transfer figure would rest on one or two attempts. At 7 of 10 the 80 % Jeffreys interval runs from 0.50 to 0.85, and at 28 of 40 from 0.60 to 0.78 (computed 2026-09-28 with `scipy.stats.beta`). The transfer dimension of the profile is therefore readable pooled across the graph or a domain, and only after months of play.

### The reserve and the frames' context tags can't be added later

Every field item 4 names can be computed afterwards from the log if the log records each task's subtype, format and context. The subtype and format are in `item_shown` today, and the context follows from `frameId` once `frame_accepted` carries the tag. Without the tag from the first frame, every accepted frame would need classifying again, and the hold couldn't run at all. The reserve is different: it is a rule about what the Director shows. A first encounter spent in training in the first weeks is gone, and no later report brings it back. The addendum's own acceptance test also asks about the first 2 weeks. So the reserve and the frames' tags have to act from her first day. The report section reads only the log and can wait. An approved requirement says a changed payload takes a new version with an upcaster, and ADR-0210 gave all of addendum 1's fields one version 2 of `item_shown` (SPC-0020, read 2026-09-28).

### Five ways to handle first encounters, and each wins at something

The owner fixed the observation and the reserve. The approved record leaves the reserve's length, the eligibility of an observation and the home of a context open, so research chose among these five.

| Option | Better at | Case against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do nothing: drop item 4, keep frames and formats as they are | no change, no field, no hold on variety, and no figure that invites over-reading | leaves the profile's transfer dimension empty, so a node that fails in a new setting looks like a node that doesn't, which the owner's falsifiability rule forbids |
| Record only: tag contexts and compute `firstExposure`, with no reserve | the smallest change that makes the log complete; training variety stays as it is | the frame rule spends new contexts and the half-bare rule brings the context format within the first few tasks, so almost every near observation falls before mastery and measures learning |
| The addendum's time rule: hold formats and contexts for the first 2 weeks | a simple rule a test can check, in the owner's words | two weeks doesn't track mastery; a node met about once a week isn't fluent in two weeks, so most held variants release while she is still learning |
| A mastery-gated hold: hold the context format and each subtype's last unshown context until its node is fluent by a tested result, the format for at most 14 game days | the held context's observation falls after mastery, one context per subtype is kept until then, and the variability evidence says little learning is lost | fewer word problems early in a subtype's life, a subtype that never becomes fluent never meets its last context, and the item builder and the frame picker gain one more rule each |
| Dedicated transfer checks: a separate purpose, planned after mastery, with frames written only for transfer | clean tests of fixed distance, like Fuchs's measures, independent of training | more frames for the parent to accept, more slots in a full day, and a second system beside the reserve for a figure that stays pooled |

Research chose the mastery-gated hold, because it is the only option that keeps one near observation per subtype for after mastery by construction while every approved rule holds. That guarantee covers the held context alone: the format observation of a node slower than 14 game days comes before mastery and is ineligible, so such a subtype gives one near observation, not two. The case against it: the hold keeps the context format from a weak subtype for up to 14 game days, and word problems are half of Cito's items (RES-4080). A slow subtype also keeps one context unseen for months, so her training misses that setting until the node is fluent. The 14-day cap on the format applies to each subtype from its own first show, so the cost repeats for every subtype introduced through the year. The owner's text reads more naturally as the game's first two weeks, and I chose the per-subtype reading because a subtype first met in month three would otherwise have no hold at all; acceptance test 2 is rewritten on that reading. A third cost comes from ranking the hold above the frame repeat rule: while a structure has only 5 frames, as at stage 0.2, a held context forces some frames to repeat within 14 game days, each flagged `frameRepeat`. For the second I found no evidence that one held setting out of several slows her learning. The evidence gives no reason to abandon the idea: Pan and Rickard find transfer real but moderate, and Barnett and Ceci show it depends on what is measured, which is exactly why a report should count it and not assume it.

## Conclusions

1. Every frame must carry exactly one context from a closed list, `content/contexts.yaml`, and the frame request of ADR-0130 must name the context it asks for, because "new context" can be decided only against a fixed list. The list may grow only by adding a tag, and a new tag never retags a frame already accepted, so no earlier show changes from used to new. A frame whose setting fits no tag waits until the parent adds one. Every template with `format: "context"` must list the contexts its structure admits in `contexts`, and the verify command must warn when a template lists fewer than 2, because one context leaves nothing to hold.
2. `frame_accepted` must record the frame's context, so the context of every show follows from `item_shown.frameId` in the log alone, and `item_shown` must gain no `context` field, because the event already names the frame and a second declaration could disagree with it.
3. The format axis of item 4 must be ADR-0290's `format`. Templates must get no `formats` list, an inverse problem must be a subtype in the graph file, and a source task must stay a task of a Sources track node.
4. For each subtype with templates in both formats, the item builder must show only bare templates from the subtype's first show until its node is «бегло» or higher by a tested result, or until 14 game days have passed since that first show, whichever comes first.
5. For a subtype with at least 2 contexts that have an accepted frame of its structure, the frame picker must not show its last unshown such context until the subtype's node is «бегло» or higher by a tested result. The hold outranks ADR-0130's rule against a repeat within 14 game days, because a repeat costs one familiar text and is flagged `frameRepeat`, while a release costs the subtype's only near observation after mastery. While a context format or context is held, the `why` field must record `transfer_hold`.
6. The projection `first_exposures` must compute `firstExposure` from the log, never as a field of `item_shown`, reading the subtype and format from `item_shown` and the context from the frame's tag through `frameId`. It must give one on the first show of a subtype, of a subtype in a format, and of a subtype in a context, each once, with `kind`, `distance`, `eligible`, `reason` when not eligible, and `expected`, the model's chance of success for that subtype at the show, replayed under the model version active then. A fact the log already holds stays out of the event, as RES-4200 rules for addendum 2, so the projection can be corrected when the list of contexts grows. `distance` must be `far` for `subtype` and `near` for `format` and `context`. A show new on several counts must get one `firstExposure` of the highest kind, subtype before format before context, and mark the other pairs as used.
7. A first encounter must be eligible only when all of these hold: it is a graded first attempt on a task with an empty `forms`; it isn't a rapid guess and doesn't carry the fatigue weight; and no lesson mark on its node falls in the 21 days before it. A walkthrough doesn't make it ineligible: a near observation follows training on its subtype by design, and a far one's earlier walkthroughs are of prerequisites, whose mastery is the condition far transfer tests. For `subtype`, each prerequisite of the node must also be «бегло» or higher by a tested result. For `format` and `context`, the node itself must be «бегло» or higher by a tested result.
8. The item builder must draw only shown subtypes and formats, and the frame picker only shown contexts, for a warm-up, a twin, a retention check, a Dutch probe presentation, a task with a non-empty `forms` and a live frame. A new one shown there is used up with no clean observation, or mixes transfer into another measurement.
9. A transfer observation must be the first attempt alone, sorted by the categories of item 3's weekly breakdown: «сама» (on her own), «на пороге» (rung 1 was enough), «с опорой» (rungs 2 to 3) and «требует обучения» (needs teaching). Only «сама», a right answer with no hint before it, counts as transferred. This widens the addendum's "without help": a hinted first attempt stays an observation of failed transfer, because she opens the ladder exactly when she can't transfer, and dropping those attempts would push the share up. A later attempt on the same subtype, format or context must never count as transfer.
10. The first-encounter attempts must keep counting in "on her own" as ordinary tasks. The projection `first_exposures` must hold the used subtypes, formats and contexts and the observations, and must feed no estimate, state, probe or block, only the Director's hold and the report.
11. The report must show near and far transfer apart, pooled across the graph and by domain, never by node. Each figure must give the share «сама», the count of eligible observations, the 80 % interval and the mean `expected` beside it, and must read «мало данных» (too little data) below 10 eligible observations. It must define near as «новый формат или сюжет знакомого подтипа» (a new format or setting of a known subtype) and far as «новый подтип, пререквизиты которого освоены» (a new subtype whose prerequisites she has mastered), and must say that the game can't see what school has taught.
12. The MVP must hold conclusions 1 to 10, because the hold works only from her first day and needs every frame's context tag from the first accepted frame. The transfer section of the report, conclusion 11, and the profile's transfer dimension must go to the post-MVP backlog, because they read only the log.
13. `frame_accepted` must gain its context as an optional field under RES-4200's rule for addendum 2's fields, and `item_shown` must gain nothing for item 4.
14. Acceptance test 2 must read: on a 60-day simulation, the projection `first_exposures` gives each subtype a `firstExposure` at most once per pair, and exactly once per pair not used up by a higher kind at the same show; no subtype with templates in both formats shows its context format before its node is fluent by a tested result or 14 game days have passed; no subtype with at least 2 contexts that have an accepted frame shows its last unshown such context before its node is fluent by a tested result; and the half-bare rule of ADR-0290 still holds on every node.
15. Each approved record below must be corrected by a new record that names what it replaces, because approved records are frozen:
    - ADR-0130 and SPC-0130, on the context of each frame, the context in the frame request, the hold in the frame picker, and only shown contexts for live frames and the other slots of conclusion 8;
    - ADR-0040 and SPC-0040, on the template's `contexts` and the item builder's draw of subtypes and formats for the slots of conclusion 8;
    - ADR-0290 and SPC-0290, on holding the context format inside the half-bare rule;
    - ADR-0070 and SPC-0070, on the `why` value `transfer_hold`;
    - ADR-0020 and SPC-0020, on the context in `frame_accepted` and the projection `first_exposures`, which computes `firstExposure`;
    - ADR-0210, on the MVP scope of conclusion 12.

### Decided on 2026-09-28

The format axis of item 4 is ADR-0290's `format`, templates get no `formats` list, inverse problems are subtypes and source tasks stay in the Sources track. A second list would duplicate a field the half-bare rule, its simulation and the report already read, and the graph and ADR-0300 already give the other two formats their homes. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

The text of a word problem comes from a frame chosen apart from the template, and the log can tell a first context only if the frame's own acceptance records it.
