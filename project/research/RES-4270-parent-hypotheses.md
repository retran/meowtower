# The parent's hypotheses test anything only if each is judged on data logged after it was written, by numeric criteria fixed in advance, and their events belong in the MVP while the computed status waits for the profile

## Summary

The owner's addendum 2 of 2026-09-28, item 8, adds a page «Гипотезы» (Hypotheses) to the Parent Room. The parent writes a hypothesis before looking at the data, with what would confirm it and what would refute it. The hypothesis links to profile dimensions and nodes, the report marks it «подтверждается» (is being confirmed), «опровергается» (is being refuted) or «мало данных» (too little data) from current data, and every change is a log event that never rewrites old wording. The approved record has no hypothesis, no parent free text evaluated against data and no parent event that changes over time, so the item adds to it without overturning a decision. It conflicts with the approved record in four places: the MVP contents list, the catalogue of parent events, the report's screen list, and the rule that a new model version recomputes every derived value. The published evidence supports the item and names its weak point. Outcome knowledge changes judgment without the judge noticing (Fischhoff, 1975), and testing after every new observation raised a nominal 5 % false-positive rate to 22 % (Simmons, Nelson and Simonsohn, 2011). A report that recomputes the label after every adventure, over data the parent has already read, reproduces both failures. Research decided eight questions on 2026-09-28: the status reads only data logged after the hypothesis or its last change of criteria, criteria are numeric conditions over a fixed list of home measures, a label needs 20 new observations per measure and holds 7 play days before any change, which a version change restarts, a met refutation outranks a met confirmation, the events and a plain form join the MVP while the computed status waits for the profile, and hypotheses never leave the Mac or reach any model. I cover item 8 and the report line «Гипотезы и их статус» (hypotheses and their status); the profile, the Dutch probe and the other items belong to their own records.

## The question

What must the game, its log and its report do so that a hypothesis the parent writes is tested by the data and never fitted to it? The owner's addendum is the instruction, dated 2026-09-28, and it outranks the approved record where they disagree.

The addendum assumes that writing a hypothesis "before looking at the data" is something the parent can do and the system can rely on. On this record that assumption fails. The report of ADR-0180 rebuilds after every adventure, and the parent reads its node cards, limits and word-problem matrix long before she writes any hypothesis, so by the time she writes one she has already looked. The game can't check what a person has seen. What it can fix is which data judges the hypothesis: the data logged after she wrote it. A second assumption hides in "shown with current data as confirmed, refuted or too little data". A label computed from free text is impossible, and a label computed from numbers the parent reads every day is the repeated testing that turns noise into a finding. So the question has three parts: which data a hypothesis is judged on, in what form its criteria must be written for a program to judge them, and when a label may move.

## Findings

### The addendum fixes the page, the advance criteria, the links, the three labels and the event rule

The owner's addendum, item 8, read 2026-09-28, sets these rules. The Parent Room gets a page «Гипотезы». The parent writes a hypothesis before looking at the data, and writes in advance what would confirm it and what would refute it. The addendum's example reads: the weakness in Cito context problems comes from language, not maths; confirmed if the gap between Russian and Dutch problems exceeds 20 percentage points and vanishes after the words are explained; refuted if Russian problems also fall 20 points below bare ones. A hypothesis links to profile dimensions and nodes. The report shows current data beside it and marks it «подтверждается», «опровергается» or «мало данных». Hypotheses and their changes are log events, `hypothesis_recorded` and `hypothesis_updated`, and past wording is never rewritten. Item 1 adds the frame: the report is never curated, every measure shows its count and an 80 % interval, a measure on little data reads «мало данных» and joins no conclusion, and hypotheses are written in advance "so that the data test them and aren't fitted to them".

### The example's criteria are numeric, but "vanishes" has no number

The example's confirmation has two conditions. The first, a gap above 20 percentage points, a program can check. The second, that the gap "vanishes after the words are explained", has no threshold: a gap of 3 points may count as vanished and a gap of 9 may not. The refutation, Russian problems 20 points below bare ones, is numeric again. All three conditions compare two presentation shares from the Dutch probe of item 6 (addendum 2, read 2026-09-28). So the example itself shows that a criterion the parent writes freely can carry a word that only a person can judge, and that the natural terms are differences between two measures against a threshold.

### Outcome knowledge raises the judged likelihood of the outcome, and the judge doesn't notice

Fischhoff gave judges event descriptions, some with a reported outcome and some without. Receiving outcome knowledge "was found to increase the postdicted likelihood of reported events and change the perceived relevance of event-descriptive data". Judges "were, however, largely unaware of the effect", and "overestimated what they would have known without outcome knowledge" (Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance, 1975, volume 1, pages 288 to 299, read 2026-09-28). A parent who has read the report and then writes a hypothesis can't tell how much the report shaped it. An instruction to "write before looking" asks for a state of mind the judge can't check in herself.

### Preregistration separates prediction from postdiction by what data judges the claim

Nosek and colleagues define preregistration as "committing to analytic steps without advance knowledge of the research outcomes". They separate prediction, which acquires new data to test a hypothesis, from postdiction, which explains data already seen. They name hindsight bias as the reason people mistake one for the other, and they ask that a change made after seeing data be reported "transparently", with what changed and why (The preregistration revolution, PNAS, 2018, volume 115, issue 11, read 2026-09-28). The Council for Exceptional Children's research brief adds that registries timestamp each plan, that a plan "can and should" be updated as the study changes "with a brief explanation of the change", and that preregistration applies to single-case research too (Brief 4, February 2022, read 2026-09-28). One child's log is a single-case study. What makes a hypothesis a prediction is therefore that it is judged on data that didn't exist when it was written, and that every change of its criteria is dated and visible.

### Testing after every new observation raised a 5 % false-positive rate to 22 %

Simmons, Nelson and Simonsohn simulated a researcher who starts with 10 observations per condition and tests after every new observation, stopping at significance or at 50. That researcher "finds a significant effect 22% of the time" at a nominal 5 % (Psychological Science, 2011, volume 22, pages 1359 to 1366, read 2026-09-28). Adding 10 observations once after a first test of 20 gave 7.7 % at p < .05. Their first two requirements for authors are to "decide the rule for terminating data collection before data collection begins" and to "collect at least 20 observations per cell". ADR-0180 rebuilds `report_cache` after every adventure (read 2026-09-28). A label recomputed at each rebuild is a test after every few observations, so the first day the interval clears the threshold by chance, the label reads «подтверждается», and the parent sees it.

### An 80 % interval clears a threshold by chance one time in ten on each side

The addendum sets the interval at 80 % (item 1, read 2026-09-28). An 80 % two-sided interval leaves 10 % on each side, so a label that moves the first time an interval lies wholly beyond a threshold is wrong about one time in ten at a single look. Under the daily looks of the finding above it is wrong more often. That figure follows from the interval's definition, not from a study.

### The approved report computes states from explicit rules over counted attempts, and a status must too

ADR-0180 builds every state, label, step and flag from an explicit rule over counted attempts, "rules the parent can check" over "probabilities she can't". It shows «мало данных» in a limit row with fewer than 3 sessions of data (read 2026-09-28). An approved requirement that ADR-0180 carries makes every node state follow from the explicit state rules, never from the model's probabilities. A hypothesis status is a new report label, so it falls under the same rule: a stated threshold, a stated count, and a stated interval over counted observations, and never `pKnow`.

### A new model version recomputes every derived value, so a status can change without new data

ADR-0060 records four versions on every estimate and snapshot, and a new model, threshold, rules or graph version starts a full recompute. It keeps the rows of earlier versions beside the new ones, and a change of the parent's school-group setting is a new model version (read 2026-09-28). A status computed from node estimates would then flip under a version change with no new play. The addendum's rule that old wording is never rewritten covers what the parent wrote, and says nothing about what the report showed her. Nothing in the log records which label she saw when she closed a hypothesis, and nothing in the report tells a label change that new play caused from one that a new version caused.

### Parent actions are log events, and the catalogue has no hypothesis event

ADR-0020 makes the append-only log the only truth, with a database that refuses to change or delete an event and an envelope carrying `seq`, `ts`, `type`, `v` and `payload` (read 2026-09-28). SPC-0020 lists every parent action as an event type, from `parent_tag_added` to `compose_labelled`, and ADR-0210 requires every new event type to have one owning decision (read 2026-09-28). The addendum's `hypothesis_recorded` and `hypothesis_updated` fit that pattern and have no owner yet. Because events are never changed, the addendum's rule that past wording stays is already how the log behaves. The projection that shows a hypothesis must keep every version of its text, and the report shows the latest one.

### The parent's text stays on the Mac under the approved rules, and the export for the school must not hold it

ADR-0100's gateway never sends report data, and ADR-0210 lists what may leave the Mac: content made without the player, her story material, the age, the one-task explanation request and a masked riddle (read 2026-09-28). A hypothesis is none of these, and its text can hold the player's name or her school, so it stays on the Mac under the approved rules without a new one. ADR-0310's export for the school reads only `school_values` and the Cito results, and its lint check `school_export_scope` fails on any other event type (read 2026-09-28). A hypothesis event is home data, so the export for the school must keep excluding it. The whole-log export of ADR-0020, which runs on the Mac only, carries it like every other event.

### The Parent Room is parent-only, and its labels come from the Russian string file

ADR-0180 serves the Parent Room only through `/api/parent/*` routes that answer 401 without a parent session opened with the PIN. It gives the player's routes schemas with no field for a state, an estimate or a percentage, and names ADR-0190's end-to-end scan as the check that her screens show none of them. It takes every label the parent reads from the Russian content file of ADR-0160, never from a model (read 2026-09-28). ADR-0160 keeps every string the player or the parent reads in a per-language file, with the Parent Room's text under `parent.*` in `content/i18n/ru.json`, so a second language adds a file and changes no code (read 2026-09-28). A hypothesis page placed in the Parent Room inherits all three rules, and its labels need `parent.*` keys.

### School values are barred from conversion into home measures, so a criterion can't mix them

ADR-0310 shows school values beside home states on two screens after the MVP and "never converts one measure into the other". An approved requirement it carries forbids stating a school level as a home state or converting either into the other (read 2026-09-28). A criterion such as "home share minus the Snappet status above 20 points" subtracts a school value from a home measure, which that rule forbids. Addendum 2, item 5, keeps Snappet and Cito data out of the knowledge model "as an external check" (read 2026-09-28). A hypothesis can still be about school results, as the addendum's example is, while its criteria read home measures only.

### The report has eight screens in v1, and no screen for hypotheses

ADR-0180 fixes report v1 at eight screens and moves the dynamics screen after the MVP. The approved MVP contents list, which ADR-0210 amends and the scope guard of ADR-0190 enforces, names "report v1 and the raw data export" and addendum 1's items (read 2026-09-28). Neither names a hypothesis page. The profile dimensions that item 8 links to come from addendum 2, item 2, which no approved record defines yet. RES-4210, a draft of 2026-09-28, is settling them; its title computes each dimension over 28 game days with Wilson intervals, while a hypothesis reads its measures over its own judging window.

### The Dutch probe the example leans on gives a first label after about 32 play days, or 57 to 107 once the probe slows

Addendum 2, item 6, plans about 4 probe tasks a day for 3 to 4 weeks across five presentations, then 1 to 2 a day (read 2026-09-28). That is about 0.8 observations per presentation a play day at first. The example's refutation compares bare and Russian problems, and its confirmation compares Russian and Dutch, so each condition needs two presentations to reach a count. At 20 new observations per presentation, the minimum Simmons and colleagues set per cell, the example's conditions open after about 25 play days, and the 7-play-day hold decided below brings the first label to about 32. If the fast phase ends after 3 weeks, at about 17 observations, the first label comes later still. After the first 3 to 4 weeks the probe drops to 1 to 2 tasks a day, 0.2 to 0.4 observations per presentation. A hypothesis written then, or one whose criteria are rewritten then, waits 50 to 100 play days for 20 observations and 57 to 107 for its first label. These figures are my estimates from the addendum's volumes, not measurements.

### Four ways to judge a hypothesis, and each wins at something

| Option | Better at | Case against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do nothing: the parent keeps her hypotheses in a notebook and reads the report | costs nothing, and no label can mislead | no date ties a hypothesis to the log, so nothing shows it came before the data; the addendum's falsifiability rule goes unmet |
| Free text, with the parent setting the label by hand beside the linked measures | no criteria language to build, and any hypothesis fits | the author judges her own prediction after seeing the outcome, which is the bias Fischhoff measured; the addendum asks the report, not the parent, to mark it |
| Numeric criteria over a fixed list of home measures, judged only on data logged after the hypothesis, with a count floor and a holding rule | a program judges it the same way every time, and the parent can check the rule | the list of measures limits what she can write, a probe hypothesis waits about 32 play days for its first label and 57 to 107 once the probe slows, and a holding rule delays a true label as well as a false one |
| A graded strength of evidence in place of three labels, such as a posterior probability that the hypothesis holds | shows how strong the evidence is, not just which side it falls on | the parent must state a prior for each hypothesis, and the addendum sets three labels; a probability also breaks the rule that report labels come from explicit rules |

I lead with numeric criteria, because it is the only option in which the report, not the author, judges the hypothesis and the judgment is a rule the parent can redo by hand. The case against it: the fixed list of measures leaves out hypotheses about things the game doesn't measure, such as sleep or a change of teacher, and the parent may give up on a page that says «мало данных» for one to three months of play. A free-text field beside the criteria keeps such a hypothesis on record, and the report says why it has no label. The evidence gives no reason to abandon the idea: preregistration is the published remedy for the two failures the item is written against.

### Three ways to fit the item into the MVP, and each wins at something

| Option | Better at | Case against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do nothing in the MVP: the whole item comes after it, with the profile | the MVP list and the scope guard stay as approved | a hypothesis the parent forms in the first weeks, before the Cito M7 result of 2027-01-15, has no dated record, and no later report can show it came first |
| The two events and a plain form in the MVP, with the computed status after it | the record date of every early hypothesis exists from the first day, at the cost of one form and two event types | the MVP list grows again, and an early hypothesis gets no label until the profile and its criteria exist |
| The whole item in the MVP | the parent sees labels from the start | the labels need the profile dimensions and the probe's presentations, which addendum 2 defines and no approved record builds; a label over measures that don't exist yet reads «мало данных» for the whole MVP |

I lead with the events and a plain form, because the date a hypothesis was written is the one fact a later report can't rebuild, while every measure its label reads can be recomputed from the log whenever the label is built. The case against it: the plain form stores criteria as text, so when the computed status arrives, each early hypothesis needs its criteria written again as numbers, and that rewrite starts its judging window afresh.

### Decided on 2026-09-28

A hypothesis is judged only on observations logged after its `hypothesis_recorded` event, or after its last `hypothesis_updated` event that changed its criteria, because a hypothesis judged on data the parent had already read is a postdiction (Nosek and colleagues) and she can't tell how much that data shaped it (Fischhoff). The report shows the same measures over the data before that point, apart and marked «до записи» (before it was written), and never uses them for the label. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

Criteria are one to three conditions for confirmation and one to three for refutation, each joined by "and". I chose at most three because every added condition needs its own 20 observations and so delays the label further, and "and" alone because an "or" gives the label several ways to fire, the flexibility Simmons and colleagues show raises false positives. A condition compares one measure, or the difference between two, with a number, over a fixed list of home measures: profile dimensions, a node's or subtype's share of unassisted first attempts, and a probe presentation's share. A free-text field beside them holds what the conditions can't say, and it never enters the label. The addendum's own example carries a word, "vanishes", that no program can judge, and ADR-0180 builds every report label from an explicit rule. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

A condition is met when the 80 % interval of its measure, or of the difference, lies wholly on its side of the number. It is not met when the interval lies wholly on the other side, and it is open otherwise, or while any measure in it has fewer than 20 observations since the judging window opened. I chose 20 because Simmons and colleagues set it as the minimum per cell; their cells hold independent participants and hers are repeated attempts by one child, so the transfer is my judgement, not their finding. The addendum sets the 80 % interval for showing a measure, and I reuse it as the test, so the label rule and the bar the parent reads agree. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

The label is «опровергается» when every refutation condition is met, whatever the confirmation shows, and «подтверждается» when every confirmation condition is met and the refutation isn't. It is «мало данных» otherwise, including when a confirmation condition clearly fails and the refutation isn't met. A met refutation outranks a met confirmation, because the parent wrote the refutation in advance and the addendum's falsifiability rule asks the report to show a weak side as clearly as a strong one. A failed confirmation stays out of the label, because it shows the prediction wasn't borne out, not that the refutation she wrote was met. The report shows each condition's own state beside the label, «выполняется» (met), «не выполняется» (not met) or «открыто» (open), so the parent sees a failed confirmation even while the label reads «мало данных». Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

A label changes, in any direction and back to «мало данных» too, only once the new state has held on each of the last 7 play days in a record of its own that the report keeps, one row per hypothesis and play day with each condition's state and the label, since ADR-0060's `node_snapshots` hold node estimates and states and none of the probe, subtype or profile measures a condition reads, and the report shows how many times the label has changed. The report rebuilds after every adventure, which is the repeated testing that took a 5 % rate to 22 % in the simulation of Simmons and colleagues. The hold applies to every transition, so the count of changes measures the evidence and not the direction the parent hoped for. I chose 7 play days because it spans a week of daily play and counts play days, not hours, so no clock enters. The hold's false-label rate is unknown: daily snapshots over growing data are strongly correlated, so seven of them may protect little more than one. The build therefore measures it on synthetic logs whose true measures sit exactly at each condition's threshold, the worst case, counting a false label on either side, and the hold lengthens by whole weeks of play days until those logs give a false label in at most 10 % of hypotheses. I chose 10 % because it is what one side of an 80 % interval gives at a single look. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

`hypothesis_updated` carries the whole new text, criteria and links with a `change` of `wording`, `criteria`, `links`, `closed` or `reopened`, and an optional reason; `reopened` keeps the judging window the hypothesis had, because its criteria didn't change. Links to nodes and dimensions only file the hypothesis on the page and never enter the label, since each condition names its own measures. A change of criteria reopens the judging window, a change of links or wording doesn't, and the report marks it «критерии изменены после записи» (criteria changed after it was written) with the date. A `closed` update records the label and the four versions the report showed at that moment, so a later version change can't alter what the parent saw when she closed it. For an open hypothesis, a change of any of ADR-0060's four versions restarts the 7-play-day hold: the recompute rewrites every past play day under the new versions at once, so rows it rewrites never count towards the 7, only play days from the change on, and a label change it causes shows as «пересчитано по новой версии» (recomputed under a new version), apart from the count of changes that new play caused. Past text stays readable in the hypothesis's history. The research brief of the Council for Exceptional Children and Nosek and colleagues both allow changes, provided each is dated and explained. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

The two event types, the page «Гипотезы» with the text, the free-text criteria and links to nodes, and the history of each hypothesis join the MVP. The numeric criteria, the links to profile dimensions and probe presentations, and the computed label come after it, with the profile of item 2. The date a hypothesis was written is the one fact a later report can't rebuild, and the addendum's first external result, the Cito M7 of 2027-01-15, is likely to arrive before the profile does. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

Hypotheses never leave the Mac, no model call on any route reads them, and the export for the school never holds them. Criteria read home measures only; a hypothesis may link a school goal for display once ADR-0310's screens exist, and that link never enters the label. This keeps the approved egress list, ADR-0310's ban on converting school values, and addendum 2's rule that school data stays an external check. Decided on 2026-09-28 by research, on the owner's instruction to process addendum 2 through to the specifications.

## Conclusions

1. The game must record each hypothesis as `hypothesis_recorded` and each change as `hypothesis_updated`, both parent events in the player's log, written only from a Parent Room session opened with the PIN. Each event carries the whole text, criteria and links, so past wording stays readable from the log alone.
2. A new decision must own both event types and add them to SPC-0020's list of parent actions, because ADR-0210 requires one owning decision for every new event type.
3. The report must judge a hypothesis only on observations logged after its record event, or after its last update that changed its criteria. It must show the same measures over earlier data apart, marked «до записи», and never use them for the label.
4. The form must not show the current values of the linked measures while a hypothesis is being written or its criteria changed, because outcome knowledge shifts judgment without the judge noticing. This buys little, since the parent has usually read the report before she writes, and the judging window of conclusion 3 is the rule that protects the test.
5. After the MVP, criteria must be one to three numeric conditions each for confirmation and refutation, each comparing one measure or the difference between two with a number, drawn from a fixed and versioned list of home measures. A free-text field may sit beside them and must never enter the label.
6. A condition must count as met only when the 80 % interval of its measure or difference lies wholly beyond its number and every measure in it has at least 20 observations in the judging window. The label must follow the rule decided above: a met refutation gives «опровергается»; a met confirmation without it gives «подтверждается»; anything else, a clearly failed confirmation included, gives «мало данных».
7. The report must keep its own record of each hypothesis's condition states and label per play day, and a label must change in any direction only after the new state has held on each of the last 7 play days in it, and a change of any of ADR-0060's four versions must restart that hold, with rows the recompute rewrites never counting towards it. The report must show beside each hypothesis the count of label changes that new play caused, the changes a version caused marked «пересчитано по новой версии» apart, each condition's state, and each measure's count and interval.
8. Every label must come from that explicit rule over counted observations and never from `pKnow`, and each label the report shows must carry the four versions of ADR-0060 it was computed under.
9. A `closed` update must record the label and the versions shown at that moment. A change of criteria must reopen the judging window and show «критерии изменены после записи» with its date; a change of links or wording, or a `reopened` update, must not.
10. Hypotheses must stay on the Mac. No request class of ADR-0100's gateway may carry a field for them, no model on any route may read them, and ADR-0310's `school_export_scope` check must keep hypothesis events out of the export for the school.
11. Criteria must read home measures only. A link to a school goal, once ADR-0310's screens exist, must be for display and must never enter a label.
12. Hypotheses, their labels and their routes must stay under `/api/parent/*` and off every player screen. The end-to-end scan of her screens that ADR-0180 assigns to ADR-0190's verify must find no hypothesis text or label.
13. The labels «Гипотезы», «подтверждается», «опровергается», «мало данных», «до записи», «критерии изменены после записи», «пересчитано по новой версии» and the three condition states must live in the Russian content file under `parent.*`, as ADR-0180 and ADR-0160 require of every Parent Room label, so an English or Dutch file can add them later.
14. The MVP contents list must gain the two event types, the page with its text form, free-text criteria and node links, and each hypothesis's history, and they must leave no trace the scope guard of ADR-0190 treats as deferred. The scope guard must gain a trace for the computed label, such as the file of home measures criteria draw on, so it can't ship before the profile. The numeric criteria, the links to profile dimensions and probe presentations and the computed label must join the post-MVP backlog with the profile.
15. The build must check on synthetic logs that the rule gives a hypothesis of a language cause «опровергается» for a synthetic player with a real maths gap, and «подтверждается» for one with a language gap.
