# The addendum's rules for keeping the game the player's own fit the split between the Director and the Master, with the 60 % task-window share kept as a reported target, while its schedule for new systems, insert-only starters, two daily routes and the free pen amend eight approved decisions, seven research records and twelve requirements

## Summary

Most of section 12 of the owner's addendum can be built on the approved design without touching the measurement: story choices, scenes without tasks, a recurring cast, protected running jokes, rereading, hidden details, rituals and the parent's checklist all live on the Master's side of the split in ADR-0110, where the Director still decides every trial and outcome. The day plan fills about 46 of the adventure's 60 minutes with the task window, because it caps story at 10 minutes and fills the rest with rooms. A 60 % cap would need the story allowance roughly doubled, so the 60 % becomes a target the report shows and the 10-minute story cap and the time plan stay. The scene options send a choice when she taps them, and the addendum wants starters that only insert text. The free-text field is closed to five fixed points, which leaves no room for the free pen after the finale. The shop, the forge, daily quests, guiding threads and Diary pages all open on the first day today, against a schedule that opens one new system a day. The Director builds one route a day, and the three-day domain window leaves about one free floor in three days, so two routes differ mostly in order and story, and they add to the daily quests and daily rewards, which the owner decided on 2026-09-28 stay every game day. The free pen has no model budget, and the approved daily caps already take $55.80 of the $60 month, so the free pen spends what the day's adventure bucket left and gets no bucket of its own. The published evidence supports the direction. Choice and personalisation raised motivation and engagement in an arithmetic activity for children aged 9 to 11, expected rewards tied to an activity undermine children's later free choice of it more than adults', and a visible broken streak lowers later engagement. Most of that evidence comes from adults or from settings other than a game like this one. This record doesn't cover risk 6 of section 12, responsiveness without sound, which another record covers, or the rest of the addendum.

## The question

What must hold for the game to stay the player's own for months: her choices change the story, the maths doesn't feel like an entry fee, and the game doesn't grow stale? The owner's addendum of 2026-09-28 answers with a list of mechanisms and an acceptance test. Research has to find which approved records they contradict and what the published evidence says about them.

The addendum assumes that interest can be designed in ahead of play, and that a set of fixed rules (two free-action points, one taskless scene, 60 %, one system a day) will hold interest for one particular child. That assumption is weaker than it looks. The risks it answers come from a review of what might go wrong, not from watching her play, since no build exists yet (the repository holds no game code on 2026-09-28). The evidence below comes mostly from adults, from preschool apps or from classroom activities, so none of it predicts which of these rules matter for her. The rule in the addendum that can test the assumption is its own: the report's "Interest" section and its warning signal. The findings on options test whether the mechanisms should come first or whether measurement should.

A second assumption is that more story time costs the measurement nothing. The day plan trades story time against graded tasks one for one, because it fills 60 minutes, so the finding on the task window tests it.

## Findings

### Felt autonomy and competence in play predict enjoyment and wanting to play again, in adult samples

Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski (2006), read 2026-09-28, report four studies. In studies 1 to 3, «perceived in-game autonomy and competence are associated with game enjoyment, preferences, and changes in well-being pre- to post-play». In study 4, a survey of online multiplayer players, «SDT's theorized needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness independently predict enjoyment and future game play». The authors expect autonomy «to be enhanced by game designs that provide considerable flexibility over movement and strategies, choice over tasks and goals, and those where rewards are structured so as to provide feedback rather than to control the player's behavior». The participants were undergraduates in studies 1 to 3 and players aged 16 to 44 in study 4, so the paper says nothing direct about a primary-school child.

### Choice and personalisation raised motivation and engagement in an arithmetic activity for children of 9 to 11

Cordova and Lepper (1996), read 2026-09-28 as the ERIC abstract, studied «72 fourth and fifth graders in one control and four experimental conditions» working on educational computer activities in arithmetic, and «all three strategies [contextualization, personalization, and provision of choices] produced increases in student motivation and in engagement in learning». The abstract doesn't say what the children chose between, how much they learned or how long the effect lasted.

### Expected rewards tied to an activity undermine children's later free choice of it more than adults', and praise doesn't raise children's free choice

Deci, Koestner and Ryan (1999), read 2026-09-28, pooled 128 experiments. Engagement-, completion- and performance-contingent rewards «significantly undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation (d = -0.40, -0.36, and -0.28, respectively)», as did all expected rewards. For 57 tangible-reward studies with children the composite was d = -0.39, against -0.27 for 38 with college students, and «verbal rewards did not enhance free-choice intrinsic motivation for children but did for college students». Unexpected and task-noncontingent tangible rewards didn't affect intrinsic motivation. The studies reward a real-world activity with real-world rewards, so the finding bears on a parent tying play to rewards or punishments outside the game. Whether the game's own chests act as such rewards isn't tested there.

### A broken streak shown in a log lowers later engagement, more when the person blames herself

Silverman and Barasch (2023), read 2026-09-28 as the publisher's abstract, found that «intact streaks highlighted via behavioral logs increase consumers' subsequent engagement» compared with broken ones, that the effect depends on how the log shows the behaviour and not on the behaviour itself, and that it grows «when individuals attribute streak breaks to personal responsibility» and shrinks when a broken streak can be restored. The samples were adult consumers.

### Apps used by young children commonly pressure them through characters, daily rewards and invented time pressure

Radesky et al. (2022), read 2026-09-28, studied the apps used by 160 children with a mean age of 4.0 years: «almost all (98.8%) had at least 1 manipulative design feature». Their categories include parasocial relationship pressure, characters discouraging a child from leaving, with the example «people are protesting your absence!», and lures such as «stickers, trophies, leaderboards, daily rewards» shown at decision points. The children were younger than the player, so the paper names the patterns to avoid more than it measures their effect on her.

### Language models trained on likelihood produce bland and repetitive text when decoded for likelihood

Holtzman et al. (2019), read 2026-09-28: «using likelihood as a decoding objective leads to text that is bland and strangely repetitive», and the decoding strategy alone changes the quality of text from the same model. The paper measures repetition inside one generated text, with the models of 2019. It doesn't measure repetition across sessions of one story, which is the risk the addendum names.

### The day plan fills about 46 of the adventure's 60 minutes with the task window, so a 60 % cap needs about 10 more minutes away from tasks

ADR-0090 plans the adventure at about 60 minutes of active time, by the owner's decision of 2026-09-26, and fills the forecast to 60 minutes with rooms after adding at most 10 minutes of story, about 1.75 minutes of eye exercises and the finale. The 10-minute story cap is a default the requirements step took from RES-1000's "8-10 minutes". RES-1000's arithmetic for the draft's 40-minute adventure sets aside 8 minutes of story, 1 of eye exercises and 2 of finale, which leaves 29 of 40 minutes, about 72 %, for tasks. The same arithmetic at 60 minutes leaves about 46 minutes, about 77 %, for the task window, which also holds the review and the second attempt (ADR-0080). The addendum's 60 % allows 36 minutes. Extensions push the share higher, because each adds only rooms (ADR-0070).

The graded floor doesn't block the cap on its own. ADR-0070 plans at least 28 graded first attempts, by the same decision of the owner, and at RES-1000's pace of 26 to 37 seconds a task, 36 tasks with reviews fit in about 28 minutes. At ADR-0090's default of 75 seconds a first attempt with its review, which applies before she has 5 days of history, 28 graded attempts alone take 35 of the 36 minutes. The warm-ups, control facts and second attempts, which the window also holds, then push it past the cap, so the first days break either the floor or the cap. What blocks the cap on later days is the story limit: with story at 10 minutes and the plan filling 60, about 10 minutes more must go to story, taskless scenes or other screens without tasks.

### Four ways to answer the interest risks compared

| Option | What its advocate would say it's better at | The case against it |

The owner's instruction decides the mechanisms, so this comparison informs only the task-window trade-off, and the last option is chosen: it keeps the 10-minute story cap, the time plan and the floor of 28 graded attempts, while the scene without tasks of conclusion 3 and the "Interest" section answer risk 1 in its place. The case against the addendum is that it designs for risks nobody has yet seen in her play, and the "Interest" section is what will show which of them were real.

## Conclusions

1. Each adventure must hold at least 2 points where her own action, typed, dictated or a starter she sends, changes what a later scene shows, and at least one of them must not be the lead-in to a trial. The change must travel through the Master's story effects and the planner's beats, and must never change a trial, its outcome or which task comes, so the measurement stays where ADR-0070 and ADR-0110 put it.
2. A non-standard action must leave a consequence she can see in a later scene of the same adventure or the next one, stored as a story-memory fact the planner reads. I chose "the same or the next adventure" as the window, because the addendum gives none and a consequence that comes later than the next day's story can no longer be told apart from chance.
3. Each adventure must hold at least one scene without tasks that is neither a trial's lead-in nor a transition, because a lead-in or a transition exists to reach a task, and the addendum's risk 1 is that the story reads as the wrapping of the maths.
4. The report must show, for each adventure, the task window's share of the adventure's active time, measured from the log as the time the task window is open over the adventure's active time, against a target of 60 %. The 60 % must stay a reported target and not a cap, and ADR-0090's 10-minute story cap, its time plan and ADR-0070's floor of 28 graded attempts stay as they are, because enforcing the cap would break the floor on the first slow days (the first decision below).
5. A tapped starter, or a number key 1 to 3, must insert its words into the empty field and never send them, and «Дальше» must keep working with an empty field. A starter of 3 to 5 words that she sends unchanged must count as her action, because the addendum says a short text is enough and a starter she chose is still her choice; but at least one of the two points of conclusion 1 in each adventure must hold text she typed, dictated or edited, so the Master's words alone can't meet the floor, and the signal of conclusion 20 watches the share of unchanged starters. Ready-made options that send must appear only where the plot itself offers a choice, such as the route, a name or where to go. This amends ADR-0110 part 4, ADR-0150, RES-1600 and RES-3200 conclusions 21 and 22, and the requirements on scene options and the number keys.
6. The free pen must open at any moment the task window isn't open, during an adventure included, as free co-writing with the Master and no task, score or knowledge-model event. It must pass the same triggers, judge and checks as every other free text in ADR-0110, show the note that parents can read the story, because the free pen is free text like any other and the approved design tells her who reads it, and be switched off by a parent setting, as the addendum requires, because the free pen adds model calls and screen time the parent may not want. The requirement that closes the free-text field to five points must be amended to add it, and ADR-0090's rule on screens after the finale must drop the 04:00 end, which stays only as the unseen game-day boundary.
7. The free pen's time must count in the day's active time and the eye count. When the day's active time passes the soft-stop point during the free pen, the free pen must close the book through a story scene and offer no extension, because «Ещё один ряд» (One more row) adds rooms with tasks, which the free pen has none of (the third decision below).
8. The free pen must spend what the day's adventure bucket of ADR-0100 left, with no bucket of its own, and must close through a story scene when that runs out, because a new bucket above about $0.035 a day lets the daily caps pass the $60 month (the second decision below). The parse bucket of $0.1 a day stays on the play key, and the daily caps on the play key must sum below $60 in a 31-day month.
9. The game must keep no streak of days and take nothing for a missed day, as the approved record already requires. Daily rewards and daily quests must come every game day once their system has opened, because the owner decided on 2026-09-28 that they keep the player coming back each day; nothing counts consecutive days, and an unmet quest vanishes without penalty (ADR-0140). The clean-row garland inside an adventure must stay as RES-1700 has it (the fourth decision below). The first scene after a gap must mention neither the gap nor her absence, and must not pressure her through a character. A content check must refuse, in the return lines, phrases of missing her, waiting for her or asking where she was, from a list the parent approves, because a character who misses her is the parasocial pressure Radesky et al. found in apps for young children.
10. Each adventure must offer two routes, both built by `planDay` under the three-day window and the rule on overdue domains, with the same planned number of graded slots. They must differ in at least one floor where the window allows and otherwise in floor order and story, and be offered as a story choice before the first floor, because even a choice of order and story is a choice over goals, which the evidence ties to motivation, and her pick tells the parent what she avoids. The first route is the one of higher total value by ADR-0070's ranking, and «Дальше» must take it, so a player who doesn't choose gets the route the Director would have built alone. The choice of routes adds to the daily quests and never replaces them. The log must record both routes and the choice. This amends ADR-0070.
11. The Parent Room's memo must keep the rule against tying play to rewards or punishments outside the game, because expected rewards tied to an activity undermine a child's later free choice of it. It must gain a checklist for the first week, because, in my judgement, the first week is when the family sets its habits around play, and the schedule of conclusion 12 opens most systems in it. This extends ADR-0180's memo panel.
12. New systems must open one a day, counted in days of play, in the addendum's order, with evolution and the Underside opening by their events, a `system_unlocked` event for each, and a parent setting that opens everything at once. Task forms don't count as systems. This amends ADR-0140 and its test 8, RES-2100 conclusion 13, ADR-0080's grant of threads and ADR-0090's Session 0, which must stop training threads, hints and explanations. The schedule delays only the day the daily quests first appear; from then on they come every game day. It also amends the requirements on the shop's first day, daily quests from the first adventure, the morning threads, the ever-present thread button and Diary pages in chests. Until threads open, every attempt is unassisted, and the free short solution after a wrong answer stays.
13. The Master's text must carry phrase memory, with the addendum's figures: sequences of 3 to 5 words, counted as lemmas so Russian inflections match, over 14 days, where a sequence used more than 3 times joins a "do not use" list in the next orders. A drafted scene that still repeats one gets one regeneration, and a reply on the free-text path gets none, so the 6-second wait holds. Openings, finales and character temperaments must come from decks, which I read as dealt without replacement, and the similarity of each opening to the last 14, the addendum's number, must be scored on the Mac with no model call, so the check costs nothing. The report must show a repetitiveness figure, and the addendum's 5 scenes drawn at random each week must enter the parent's review queue. This amends ADR-0110.
14. The running jokes in story memory, the canon's catchphrases and the names she gave must be on a whitelist the freshness filter never strikes, and each running joke must still appear at most once a session, as CAN-0010 requires.
